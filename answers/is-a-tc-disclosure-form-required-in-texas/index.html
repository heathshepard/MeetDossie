Is a Transaction Coordinator Disclosure Form Required in Texas?

Not yet — here's exactly what TREC has (and hasn't) done, and when to check back.

Updated 2026-08-09 By Heath Shepard, Texas REALTOR®

Short answer

No. As of August 9, 2026, there is no Texas transaction coordinator (TC) disclosure form, and nothing is required of any agent, broker, or TC today. TREC's Broker Responsibility Advisory Committee (BRAC) asked its Broker-Lawyer Committee, at an April 8, 2026 meeting, to investigate developing a consumer disclosure form about TCs — including whether a TC is licensed or unlicensed. That's a request to study an idea, not a rule. BRAC picked the topic back up on July 14, 2026 and then tabled it until its next meeting, October 7, 2026. Nothing has been proposed, drafted, or adopted. Treat this as a proposal to watch, not a requirement to comply with.

What actually happened, in order

This traces back to Texas Real Estate Commission's Broker Responsibility Advisory Committee (BRAC) — a standing TREC advisory committee, not a rulemaking body itself. Here's the exact sequence, sourced from TREC's own published meeting recaps:

  • April 8, 2026 — BRAC meeting. The committee took public comment expressing concern about the growing use of transaction coordinators, particularly unlicensed ones — TCs handling sensitive contract and financial information, TCs working across multiple brokerages with unclear supervision lines, and general consumer-protection questions. In response, BRAC asked TREC's Broker-Lawyer Committee to investigate the possible development of a consumer disclosure form about transaction coordinators, including identifying whether a transaction coordinator is licensed or unlicensed. That's a referral asking another committee to study an idea — not a vote to create a form, and not a rule.
  • April 10, 2026 — Broker-Lawyer Committee meeting. The committee acknowledged transaction coordinator considerations as one of several broader issues flagged for future discussion, alongside unrelated topics like time-of-the-essence clauses. No disclosure form was drafted or proposed at this meeting.
  • July 14, 2026 — BRAC meeting. The committee picked the topic back up, continuing its discussion of transaction coordinators and possible ways to improve consumer transparency. After hearing public input and discussing potential disclosure options, BRAC agreed to table the topic until its next meeting.
  • October 7, 2026 — BRAC's next scheduled meeting. This is the earliest point where further movement — including whether the Broker-Lawyer Committee has produced any draft language — is likely to surface. Nothing is scheduled to take effect on this date; it is simply when the committee is expected to revisit the topic.

What this means today

Nothing. There is no TREC-promulgated form, no proposed rule text, and no compliance deadline tied to this discussion. A transaction coordinator working in Texas today — licensed or unlicensed — operates under the same rules that applied before April 2026. If you're an agent, broker, or TC wondering whether you need to start disclosing anything new: you don't, because nothing has been adopted.

Why this is still worth watching. The underlying concern — that consumers often don't know whether the person coordinating their transaction is a licensed real estate professional or an unlicensed administrative worker — is a real and recurring topic across multiple 2026 BRAC meetings, not a one-off comment. If TREC eventually does propose a form, it would likely follow the same path SB 1968 and the IABS 1-2 update did: public comment, committee drafting, a formal proposal, and an adoption date announced well in advance. That process has not started yet for this topic.

Do unlicensed transaction coordinators have to disclose their license status to clients in Texas today? +
There's no TREC-mandated disclosure form requiring this today. Separately, an unlicensed TC is already restricted under existing Texas rules to ministerial tasks and cannot hold themselves out as a licensed agent — but that's a distinct, existing rule, not the new consumer disclosure form BRAC is studying.
