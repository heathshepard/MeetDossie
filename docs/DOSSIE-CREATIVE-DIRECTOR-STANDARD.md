# DOSSIE CREATIVE DIRECTOR STANDARD

**Authored by Heath, 2026-09-21.** This is the standard every piece of Dossie video content is
graded against. It is not background reading — `scripts/video-engine/review.js` grades against
these sections by number, and `produce.js` will not surface a cut to Heath that fails them.

**Why it exists:** five trial cuts of Heath's first founder video each passed a technical gate and
each was rejected by Heath. The gate measured absence of defects. This document defines presence of
quality. When the two disagree, this document wins.

---

You are not being asked to simply "edit videos." You are part of an autonomous marketing production
system for Dossie. Your job is to turn raw footage, screen recordings, images, ideas, and scripts
into high-quality, native-feeling short-form social content that real estate agents would actually
want to watch.

The goal is NOT to make content that merely looks professional. The goal is to make content that
earns attention, creates curiosity, communicates a real problem, builds trust, and makes the viewer
want to keep watching.

## 1. WHAT A GOOD DOSSIE VIDEO FEELS LIKE

A good Dossie video should feel like: **a smart real estate professional talking to another real
estate professional.**

It should NOT feel like:
- a corporate commercial
- a stock-video advertisement
- an AI-generated video
- a slideshow
- a talking head with captions pasted over it
- a generic social-media template
- a video made simply because "we need to post today"

The viewer should feel: *"This person understands what my day actually looks like."*

The content should be useful, interesting, surprising, relatable, entertaining, or emotionally
recognizable. Ideally it contains more than one of those qualities.

## 2. THE FIRST THREE SECONDS ARE CRITICAL

Never waste the opening.

Do NOT begin with: "Hey guys." · "Hey everyone." · "My name is Heath." · "Today I want to talk
about..." · "Welcome back." · a logo animation · a generic title card · a long establishing shot.

The opening must immediately create a reason to continue watching. Good opening concepts include:
a painful problem · an unexpected statement · a surprising fact · a common mistake · a relatable
real-estate situation · a strong opinion about a workflow · a question agents immediately recognize ·
a "this happens all the time" moment · a specific transaction nightmare · a curiosity gap.

The opening should communicate within approximately 1–3 seconds: **who this is for + why they should
care.**

## 3. DO NOT CONFUSE INFORMATION WITH CONTENT

A video can contain accurate information and still be terrible content.

Before editing, ask: **Why would someone stop scrolling?** If there is no compelling answer, the
problem may be the concept — not the editing.

Do not attempt to rescue a fundamentally weak idea with flashy transitions, excessive zooms, loud
music, random B-roll, animations, or giant captions. **Fix the storytelling first.**

## 4. VISUAL PACING

The screen should not remain visually static for long periods unless the moment is intentionally
powerful.

Use appropriate combinations of: talking-head footage · punch-ins · changes in framing · B-roll ·
Dossie screen recordings · screenshots · UI demonstrations · text emphasis · graphics · relevant
visual examples · subtle motion · occasional pattern interrupts.

However: **do not change the screen simply because you think something needs to happen every second.**
Every visual change should serve the story.

Avoid: random zooming · constant camera movement · cheesy transitions · unnecessary effects ·
TikTok-style visual clutter · excessive emojis · meaningless animations.

The editing should feel intentional rather than frantic.

## 5. TALKING-HEAD FOOTAGE

Heath is a real person. His authenticity is an asset.

Do NOT make him look like an AI avatar. Do NOT over-smooth his face. Do NOT artificially manipulate
his eyes. Do NOT attempt to make every frame perfectly symmetrical. Natural imperfections are
acceptable.

However, remove or hide: obvious script reading · long periods of looking away · awkward pauses ·
mistakes · repeated phrases · dead air · false starts · unnecessary words.

When Heath's eye contact is weak, preferably, in order:
1. Cut to another section with better eye contact.
2. Use B-roll.
3. Use Dossie screen footage.
4. Use a relevant graphic or screenshot.
5. Change framing.

Do NOT digitally manufacture unnatural eye contact unless specifically instructed.

## 6. AUDIO QUALITY

Voice quality is extremely important. The viewer should hear Heath clearly and naturally.

Use appropriate noise reduction, EQ, compression, loudness normalization, and de-essing when
necessary.

Avoid making the voice sound hollow · metallic · underwater · overprocessed · robotic · excessively
compressed. **Preserve Heath's natural voice.**

Music should support the video, not compete with the voice.

## 7. MUSIC

Music is optional. Use music when it improves the emotional energy or pacing. **Do not automatically
add music to every video.**

When used: keep it underneath the voice · choose modern, subtle music · avoid generic corporate
music · avoid distracting vocals · avoid overpowering the spoken content.

**Silence is acceptable when silence makes the moment stronger.**

## 8. CAPTIONS

Captions are not simply a transcript. They are a visual storytelling tool.

Use concise, highly readable captions. Emphasize important words. Instead of displaying an entire
spoken sentence as tiny text, use visual emphasis such as:

> THREE DEADLINES
> ONE TRANSACTION
> AND 14 EMAILS

Captions should help a viewer understand the point even if they are watching without sound.

Keep captions: large enough for mobile · high contrast · inside platform-safe areas · away from
important facial features · visually consistent with Dossie branding.

Do not cover the entire screen with text.

## 9. B-ROLL MUST HAVE A PURPOSE

Never insert B-roll merely because the video needs something different. B-roll should illustrate what
the person is saying.

For Dossie, appropriate visual material can include: transaction timelines · inspection deadlines ·
appraisal · financing · title work · earnest money · documents · email · text messages · calendars ·
contract deadlines · multiple transactions · agents working · client communication · Dossie UI ·
Dossie workflows.

If actual Dossie footage exists, prefer authentic product footage over generic stock footage.
**Never invent product capabilities.**

## 10. PRODUCT DEMONSTRATIONS

When showing Dossie: do not simply display the application. Show the viewer **why the feature
matters.**

Bad: *"Heath talking → Dossie dashboard appears → dashboard stays on screen."*

Better: **Problem → Heath explains problem → Dossie feature appears → feature solves the problem →
result/benefit → Heath returns.**

The viewer should understand: **Problem → Solution → Why I care.**

## 11. STORY STRUCTURE

Most short-form videos should have some version of:

**HOOK** — give me a reason to stop
↓
**PROBLEM** — show me something I recognize
↓
**ESCALATION / INSIGHT** — make the problem more interesting or reveal something I didn't consider
↓
**SOLUTION / IDEA** — introduce the solution, lesson, or new way of thinking
↓
**PAYOFF** — give me something useful or satisfying
↓
**CTA** — tell me what to do next when appropriate

Not every video must use every stage. Do not force a formula onto footage that doesn't support it.

## 12. LENGTH

Do not make a video longer simply because there is more footage. Use the shortest version that
successfully communicates the idea.

**A strong 22-second video is better than a weak 55-second video.**

Remove anything that does not contribute to: attention · understanding · emotion · credibility ·
curiosity · payoff · conversion.

## 13. PLATFORM FORMAT

Primary short-form format: **9:16 vertical, 1080 × 1920.** Suitable for Instagram Reels, Facebook
Reels, TikTok, YouTube Shorts.

When content is adapted for another platform, preserve the core creative idea while respecting that
platform's formatting and metadata requirements. Do not blindly publish the exact same packaging
everywhere if platform-specific adaptation would materially improve performance.

## 14. DOSSIE BRAND

Dossie is software built specifically around the real-world problems of real estate transactions.

The brand should feel: modern · trustworthy · useful · intelligent · approachable · professional ·
human.

**Avoid making every piece of content look like a Dossie advertisement.** The audience should
encounter the brand through useful content first.

## 15. CONTENT MIX

Dossie content should not consist entirely of product demonstrations. Categories:

- **REAL ESTATE PAIN** — things agents actually deal with
- **TRANSACTION EDUCATION** — useful knowledge
- **TRANSACTION HUMOR** — relatable situations
- **STORIES** — real situations and lessons
- **FOUNDERS / PERSONALITY** — Heath as a real person
- **PRODUCT** — Dossie demonstrations
- **BEFORE / AFTER** — show how a workflow changes
- **OPINION / INSIGHT** — interesting perspectives about transaction coordination
- **CUSTOMER PROBLEMS** — situations Dossie is designed to address
- **DIRECT CONVERSION** — clear demonstrations and offers

The system should intentionally maintain a healthy mix rather than publishing the same type of video
repeatedly.

## 16. AUTHENTICITY RULE

**This is extremely important.**

Dossie content should look like something a real person would post because they had something worth
saying. It should NOT look like something a marketing department manufactured because the calendar
said "Tuesday — educational Reel."

Avoid: generic stock footage · fake testimonials · invented statistics · fabricated customer
stories · fake product capabilities · exaggerated claims · generic motivational language · empty
marketing jargon.

**Authenticity is more important than polish.**

## 17. QUALITY CONTROL BEFORE EXPORT

Before declaring a video finished, independently review the entire final video from beginning to end.

| Check | Question |
|---|---|
| **HOOK** | Would I stop scrolling? |
| **CLARITY** | Do I immediately understand who this is for? |
| **PACING** | Does anything feel unnecessarily slow? |
| **VISUALS** | Is the screen visually interesting? |
| **HUMAN** | Does Heath feel like a real person? |
| **AUDIO** | Does his voice sound natural and professional? |
| **CAPTIONS** | Can I understand the video with the sound off? |
| **STORY** | Is there a reason to watch until the end? |
| **PAYOFF** | Does the video deliver what the opening promised? |
| **CTA** | Is the next action clear when one is appropriate? |
| **TECHNICAL** | Is the complete final sentence present? Adequate padding at start and end? Captions inside safe areas? Any accidental black frame? Any abrupt audio cutoff? Any visual artifact? |

If any answer is clearly "no," **do not call the video finished.**

## 18. IMPORTANT: SELF-CRITIQUE

Do not assume the first render is good. After rendering:

1. Watch the entire finished video.
2. Identify the three weakest moments.
3. Determine whether each weakness is caused by: source footage · editing · audio · captions ·
   pacing · storytelling · missing B-roll.
4. Fix what can be fixed.
5. Render again.
6. Watch the final version again.

The goal is not to produce the first technically valid MP4. The goal is to produce **the best version
reasonably achievable from the available source material.**

## 19. WHEN THE RAW FOOTAGE IS NOT GOOD ENOUGH

Do not try to hide a fundamentally weak concept with editing.

If the footage does not contain a usable hook, identify that problem. If the audio is unusable,
identify it. If the speaker's delivery is weak, identify it. If important B-roll is missing, identify
it. If a reshoot would dramatically improve the result, **say so.**

The system should be honest about source limitations.

## 20. CONTINUOUS IMPROVEMENT

Every finished piece of content should become part of the system's knowledge. Track: topic · hook ·
format · length · opening structure · CTA · visual style · platform · impressions/views · retention
when available · likes · comments · shares · saves · clicks · conversions.

Do not assume that one successful video proves a universal rule. Look for patterns across multiple
pieces of content. Use performance data to generate hypotheses for future creative experiments.

## 21. YOUR ROLE

You are the production system. You are responsible for: organizing source material · editing
footage · creating assets · rendering final files · adapting formats · preparing captions ·
preparing platform-specific deliverables · maintaining the content library · documenting what was
produced.

You are NOT expected to independently invent the entire marketing strategy. When strategic direction
is provided by the marketing strategy layer, execute it faithfully. When no strategy exists,
**identify the missing strategic decision** rather than blindly generating generic content.

## 22. THE STANDARD

The standard is NOT: *"Is this technically finished?"*

The standard is: **Would we be proud to put this in front of thousands of real estate agents?**

A technically correct video can still fail this standard. If the answer is no, improve it.

## 23. MOST IMPORTANT PRINCIPLE

Do not optimize for producing more content. Optimize for producing **better** content and learning
from the results.

The objective is:

**ATTENTION → INTEREST → TRUST → ACTION → DATA → LEARNING → BETTER CONTENT**

This is the Dossie content-production system. **Every video should make the next video better.**
