# Fable Brief — Dossie Brokerage Compliance Submission

**Purpose:** input package for Fable to spec the capability that gets a Dossie member paid — submitting a closed file's documents to their brokerage's compliance system, through to commission disbursement.

**This is not the spec.** It is the evidence, the architecture question, and the blind spots. Fable writes the spec.

**Status of claims:** file paths and code references below were verified against the repo on 2026-09-11. Database facts were verified by live query. Items marked *(inferred)* are reasoning, not observation.

---

## Part 0 — Why this capability is the product

Submitting a file to compliance is the single most delegated, most deadline-bound, most tedious part of a TC's job. Done wrong, the agent's commission is held. It is the Door-A pitch (agent replacing a TC) made concrete, and it is the thing a member would most obviously pay for.

It is also the capability where Dossie can be *demonstrably* better than a human TC, because the failure modes are informational — knowing what order to do things in, reading a return comment nobody reads, noticing that a button was never pressed. Those are exactly the things software wins at and tired humans lose at.

## Part 1 — Lived evidence

Everything in this section happened on one real transaction: **104 Wild Cherry Ln, Boerne TX**, closed 2026-09-09 at **$760,000**. KW Command opportunity **O91-14116577**, transaction ID **6089835**. Listing side, 2.5% = **$19,000** gross, $3,130 to office (Company $1,900 / Royalty $1,140 / E&O $90), **$15,870 net to agent**.

A spec written from the abstract problem returns generic advice. These specifics are what make the problem legible.

### 1.1 The dependency loop — the core discovery

The Closed checklist has five slots. Two of them cannot be filled by the member at the time the checklist asks for them:

| Slot | Required | Who produces it |
|---|---|---|
| Final/Closing Statement | Yes | Title company |
| Disbursement Authorization | Yes | **Unclear — see 1.5** |
| Buyer's Walk-Through and Acceptance | Optional | Buyer's agent |
| MLS Agent Report showing "Sold" | Yes | The member |
| COMMISSION BREAKDOWN | Yes | **MCA, after the commission worksheet is submitted** |

The Commission Breakdown is generated by the Market Center Administrator **only after** a separate, differently-located action: `Offers & Commissions → Manage Commission → Submit`. That worksheet sat with status **"Open"** and an unpressed teal Submit button for two days after closing.

**Nothing in the interface said so.** The member waited on the brokerage; the brokerage waited on the member. The loop was invisible from both ends.

**This is the hardest and most valuable thing to encode.** Matching documents to slots is mechanical. Knowing that slot #5 cannot exist until an action on a different tab has fired is what a good TC knows and a bad one doesn't.

### 1.2 Returns carry instructions nobody reads

On 2026-09-09 the Under Contract folder was returned. The reason was attached as a comment on a *specific version* of a *specific document*, visible only under that document's Review tab after selecting that version:

> "Please revise our broker license number on page 11 to read 547594 and please add licensed supervisor on Page 11 to read Heather Elizondo and revise the license number to read lic #680541 and add our office phone number as well - No initials needed. Thanks Christie McCoy"

Three system notices fired (8/31, 9/1, 9/9) and none carried the reason. The member only learned what was wrong by clicking three levels deep.

**Requirement implied:** Dossie reads return comments, surfaces them in plain language, and ideally maps them to the specific field on the specific page.

### 1.3 Only fully executed documents go in slots

On 2026-08-30, an automated run uploaded a Seller's Disclosure Notice and an On-Site Sewer disclosure into their placeholders while the buyer receipt sections were blank and per-page buyer initials were missing. It flagged the gap in its own report and uploaded anyway. Heath deleted both by hand.

**The rule:** a document enters a compliance placeholder only when every signature and initial the form requires, from every party, is present. Not "signed by our side." Not "signed except the receipt."

**An empty slot is honestly incomplete and visible as such. A half-filled one looks done, passes a glance, and either gets bounced or — worse — gets submitted and creates a compliance problem on a file about to close.**

**Verification method matters:** e-signature marks are image overlays. Text extraction misses them entirely and has produced false readings in *both* directions on these files. Pages must be rendered and read visually.

### 1.4 Some clicks must stay human

Three actions are compliance attestations made under the member's licence:

- **"Submit to MC"** on a document folder
- **"Create & Accept Offer"** on the Offers tab
- **"Submit"** on the Manage Commission worksheet

Dossie prepares everything up to these. A human presses them. This boundary was set deliberately on 2026-08-29 and should survive into the product, not just the scripts.

### 1.5 The system lies by omission

Hovering the info icons in the Closed folder revealed that **Commission Breakdown** carries the tooltip "Note: MCA will add." **Disbursement Authorization has no icon and no note** — Command presents it as a plain required upload.

So the member cannot tell, from the interface, whether the DA will arrive automatically or must be obtained from title. As of this writing that question is still unresolved and requires asking a human (Ryan Duran, MCA at KW City View).

**Requirement implied:** Dossie must model *who owns the next step* for every slot — member, brokerage, title, or other party — because the source system does not reliably say.

### 1.6 The canonical KW flow

From Joe Sloan (Broker of Record, KW City View), emailed 2026-08-26:

```
Command → Opportunities → Create Opportunity → fill fields
  → Documents tab → Checklists dropdown → Residential
  → placeholders populate → upload each doc into its matching placeholder
  → "Submit to MC" per folder
  → then Offers tab for the commission request
  → compliance reviews → on pass, pushes to MCA for commission/DA processing
```

Folders are staged: **Listed**, **Under Contract**, **Closed** — each with its own checklist and its own Submit.

MCA contact at KW City View: Ryan Duran, mca@lifeatkwcv.com.

### 1.7 Secondary observations from the same file

- The opportunity phase remained **"Under Contract / Clear to Close"** two days after funding. The pipeline showed Closed: 0. Phase advancement is a separate manual step nobody prompts.
- A question emailed on 9/8 asking whether the DA was being prepared went to `no-reply@system.kw.com` — a system address. It was never read. *(The member had replied to a notification rather than composing to a person.)*
- The KW Workspace tenant has been rejecting some inbound mail with `550 5.7.1 Message rejected due to administrative security policy`. **Absence of an email is therefore not evidence that nothing was sent.** Any design that relies on email notification as a signal is unsafe here.

## Part 2 — The architecture question

### 2.1 The stated requirement

Per CLAUDE.md §1, Dossie is a **vertical-agnostic AI core plus a per-jurisdiction/per-brokerage config layer**. That rule applies here:

- **Core:** take a file's documents, match them to a required-document checklist, verify execution, upload, submit, track review state, surface returns.
- **Config layer:** the checklist → placeholder mapping, the action ordering, the submit flow, and the selectors or affordances for one brokerage platform.

### 2.2 The real targets

KW Command is the reference build because it is the one Heath knows firsthand. The others, roughly in order of where non-KW Texas brokerages land:

**eBrokerHouse · SkySlope · Dotloop · Brokermint · Lone Wolf**

Note Dotloop is owned by Zillow, which matters strategically — see Part 3.11.

### 2.3 The central design question, stated sharply

**How does a new market center get onboarded?**

Heath's framing: *"a way of inputting the instructions provided by other market centers on how they do it, and Dossie has scaffolding in place to adapt to each market center's methods."*

That is the right instinct but it hides a fork with very different costs. At least four mechanisms are plausible:

| Mechanism | What it is | Cost | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Declarative config** | A per-brokerage JSON/YAML: slot names, selectors, action order | Low per brokerage, high authoring skill | Brittle to UI change; requires an engineer per platform |
| **Guided setup** | The member walks Dossie through one file once; Dossie records the mapping | Medium | Depends on the member doing it correctly; one member's variation becomes canon |
| **Document ingestion** | An LLM reads the brokerage's own written procedures (like Joe Sloan's email) and derives the flow | Low marginal | Written procedures are incomplete — Joe's email did not mention the commission-worksheet dependency |
| **Observed learning** | Dossie watches a real submission and infers the flow | High | Needs many examples; hard to validate |

**The honest answer is probably a hybrid**, and Fable should be asked to choose and defend one rather than list them. A plausible shape *(inferred)*: written procedures bootstrap a draft config, a guided first-run confirms and corrects it, and the config is versioned per market center with drift detection.

**Do not pre-commit Fable to an answer. Give it the question and the evidence.**

### 2.4 The unit of configuration is not obvious

Heath says "market center." That may be too granular or not granular enough:

- **Per platform** (KW Command) — the UI and mechanics
- **Per market center** (KW City View vs KW Boerne) — which documents are required, who the MCA is, local policy
- **Per member** — their credentials, their licence, their broker

These are three different layers and they change on different schedules. A Texas TC serving agents across several brokerages needs all three to compose. Heath himself is at **KW City View and KW Boerne** — two market centers, one platform.

## Part 3 — Blind spots

Heath explicitly asked for what he hasn't thought of. These are ordered by how much they would change the build.

### 3.1 Authentication is the hardest problem and it was not named

Every member's Command sits behind their own SSO. This determines the entire architecture and nothing else can be decided first.

**Path A — stored per-member credentials.** Dossie logs in as the member via Playwright. This *works*; it was done for Heath's zipForm on 2026-09-10 (credentials in a file outside the repo, read at runtime, proven across two cold starts). But holding hundreds of agents' brokerage credentials is a serious liability: a breach exposes not just Dossie but every member's brokerage account and their clients' transaction data. It also likely violates the brokerage's own terms.

**Path B — an extension running in the member's own already-authenticated session.** No credentials stored, no liability, and it generalizes across platforms without per-platform auth work. **Heath has a working proof of this pattern on his own screen today** — a Claude-in-Chrome extension driving Command alongside a Claude Code session.

**Known constraint that shapes this:** the Claude-in-Chrome extension **cannot drive the OS file picker**. That is precisely why uploads currently use Playwright's `setInputFiles`, which needs no dialog. So Path B needs an answer for file upload specifically — possibly drag-and-drop into a drop zone, possibly a hybrid where the extension handles navigation and a local helper handles file input.

**This fork must be resolved before anything else is specified.**

### 3.2 Liability when Dossie submits wrong

Compliance submission is a licensed activity attached to the member's licence and their broker's. If Dossie uploads a document to the wrong slot, or submits an incomplete folder, or attests to something inaccurate:

- Who is responsible — the member, Dossie, or both?
- Does the ToS need to address it? *(Hadley question, not a Fable question, but the spec should flag where the boundary sits.)*
- Does this argue for keeping *more* actions human than the current three?

This is a reason the attesting-click boundary (1.4) should be treated as load-bearing, not a convenience.

### 3.3 Brokerages change their UI without notice

A DOM-driven integration breaks silently. This exact failure mode cost two days this week in a different system: a profile-collision check ran a query that a shell layer corrupted, the error was swallowed by a catch, and the check **always returned "no holder"** — it failed *open* for an unknown length of time.

**Requirement implied:** the integration must detect its own breakage and fail *closed*, loudly, rather than reporting success. Canary checks, expected-element assertions, and a "I could not find the thing I expected" state that reaches the member.

### 3.4 The ordering knowledge is the actual product — and it must be learnable

Section 1.1 is not a KW quirk. Every brokerage platform will have some version of "X cannot happen until Y." That knowledge:

- is not in the UI
- is not in the written procedures (Joe Sloan's email omitted it)
- is learned by TCs through painful experience

**How is it encoded per brokerage, and how is it acquired for a brokerage nobody at Dossie has used?** This may be the single hardest question in the spec, and it is the one that determines whether the product is a file-uploader or a transaction coordinator.

*(Inferred)* One tractable approach: model each slot with a `blocked_by` relation and a `produced_by` party, then let observed returns teach the graph — a bounce is a labelled training signal.

### 3.5 What happens on a return

Someone must read the comment, fix the document, and resubmit. Today that is three levels of clicking and a human who knows where to look.

- Does Dossie fix it, or tell the member what to fix?
- A page-11 licence-number correction is mechanical and Dossie could do it. A "the buyer never initialled page 8" is not.
- How is the member told, in plain language, and where?
- Return handling is a loop, not an event. The state machine must model resubmission cycles.

### 3.6 Document provenance — similar names, different documents

Dossie must know which document satisfies which slot:

- the **executed** contract, not the draft
- the **receipted** contract (title has acknowledged earnest money and option fee), not the executed one
- the **final signed** settlement statement, not the draft CD the member approved by email

On the Wild Cherry file, the "Receipted Contract" slot contained the fully executed TREC contract — which never had a title receipt block in it at all — while a separate optional "Contract Receipted Page" slot sat empty. The names do not reliably indicate the content.

**Existing partial support:** the `documents` table has `document_type`, `form_type`, and `signature_status` columns. See 3.10 for the problem with that last one.

### 3.7 Timing, SLA, and chasing

Commission is held until compliance passes. That is the member's money.

- What is the expected turnaround, and does it vary by market center?
- Does Dossie chase a stalled file, and after how long?
- **The specific failure to prevent:** the member waiting on the brokerage while the brokerage waits on the member, with nothing on screen distinguishing the two. Dossie must always be able to answer "whose move is it?"

### 3.8 Concurrency against a single member session

On 2026-09-11 Heath had a browser extension parked on the Command page while an automated agent worked the same browser profile. Earlier the same week, two automated processes deadlocked on one Chrome profile, and a third destroyed an authenticated session by force-killing it.

A member using Dossie *while* Dossie works their Command session is not an edge case — it is the normal case, because Dossie must hand off the attesting clicks (1.4). The design must assume shared, contended access to one authenticated session.

### 3.9 Cost per file has never been measured

Every document scan and every LLM compliance audit bills the Anthropic API directly. A 24-page contract scan is a large multimodal call, and quality testing has shown that smaller models do the audit meaningfully worse — so the expensive model is load-bearing, not a lazy default.

Members pay **$29–79/month**. A compliance submission touches many documents per file. **Nobody has measured cost per file or per member.** This affects:

- gross margin at current pricing
- the scheduled price increase to $149/$349
- whether a cheaper model can handle mechanical extraction while the expensive one handles judgment

The spec should include a cost model, not just an architecture.

### 3.10 A verified gap: `signature_status` exists and is never populated

The `documents` table has a `signature_status` column — exactly the field that would gate "only fully executed documents may enter a compliance slot" (§1.3).

**Live query, 2026-09-11: all 312 rows have `signature_status = 'none'`. The column has never been populated.**

So the enforcement mechanism for the most important safety rule in this capability has a schema and no data behind it. Any spec that assumes execution status is known is assuming something that does not currently exist. *(Related: `scripts/` contains a `signature-verifier.js` that has already caught a provider-reported "complete" envelope that was actually partial — the capability exists but is not wired to this field.)*

### 3.11 There is already a `compliance_sends` table — and it is a different model

**Live query: `compliance_sends` exists with columns `transaction_id, user_id, sent_at, sent_to_email, document_count, resend_message_id, error`. It contains exactly one row, dated 2026-05-07.**

That is an **email-based** compliance submission — bundle the documents, email them to a compliance address, log the Resend message ID. Used once, four months ago, never since.

This matters for three reasons:

1. Fable should not respec a concept that already has a table, even a vestigial one.
2. **Email submission may be the right answer for some brokerages.** Not every brokerage has a portal; some genuinely do want a PDF bundle emailed. The scaffolding should treat "email the documents" as one valid submission *strategy* alongside "drive the portal," not as a legacy artifact.
3. It is evidence of the pattern that recurs throughout this codebase: a capability gets built, is never routed to a member surface, and is forgotten. The spec should say how this one avoids that fate.

### 3.12 Strategic: Zillow owns Dotloop

Dotloop is one of the target platforms and Zillow owns it. Zillow is also publicly pushing AI into agent workflows (a Zillow/OpenAI co-marketing piece landed this week). Their current AI focus is top-of-funnel — leads, messaging, coaching — while this capability is bottom-of-funnel.

That gap is Dossie's moat today. It is worth naming in the spec because it argues for depth here rather than breadth elsewhere, and because building *on* Dotloop while competing with its owner is a real consideration.

### 3.13 The member may not be the agent

Door B of the business is TCs scaling solo. A TC submits on behalf of *multiple* agents, at *multiple* brokerages, using *their own* login or the agent's. That changes:

- whose credentials or session is used
- whose licence the attestation is made under
- how the config layers compose (3.2.4)

If the spec only models "member = agent = licence holder," it breaks for half the intended market.
