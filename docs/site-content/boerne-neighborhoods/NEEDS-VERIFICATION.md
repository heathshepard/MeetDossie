# Needs Verification — Boerne Neighborhood Guides

Open questions across the 39 neighborhood pages, ranked by how much a buyer could lose by acting on the wrong answer. Nothing in this file has been published as fact on any page; every item below is either flagged inline on its page or omitted entirely.

**Last reviewed:** September 2026.

---

## Tier 1 — Publish nothing further until resolved

### 1. Trails of Herff Ranch working capital charge: "15% of the sale price"
**Page:** `trails-of-herff-ranch.md`
The filed TREC management certificate for the Homeowners Association of Herff Ranch in Boerne, Inc. states a working capital charge of **the greater of $100 or 15% of the sale price**, due from the purchaser at closing.

Fifteen percent of the sale price would be roughly $58,000 on a median-value home here. That is far outside any normal working-capital structure (peers charge $250 to $2,000). Either the provision is applied differently in practice, or it is a drafting error in the filing.

**To resolve:** Call RealManage and get the actual current working capital figure in writing. Also pull the recorded declaration to see what it actually says.
**Risk if wrong:** A five-figure surprise at closing, or a page that materially misstates the cost of buying there.

### 2. Comal County portions of Fair Oaks Ranch — school district
**Page:** `fair-oaks-ranch.md`
The Fair Oaks Ranch master HOA certificate identifies Units C-1 through C-15, the Enclave, and SF-1 through SF-4 as **Comal County**. Secondary sources suggest those may be zoned to **Comal ISD** (Rahe Bulverde Elementary, Spring Branch Middle, Smithson Valley High) rather than Boerne ISD.

We verified Boerne ISD for the Bexar and Kendall portions only. The Comal portion is unverified and is **not asserted** on the page.

**To resolve:** Take a specific Comal-side address to both Boerne ISD and Comal ISD and get written confirmation.
**Risk if wrong:** A family buys for Boerne ISD and enrolls in a different district.

### 3. Comal County portion of Silver Hills — school district
**Page:** `silver-hills.md`
Silver Hills genuinely straddles the Kendall/Comal line — confirmed by two separately filed 2025 management certificates for the same association, one recorded in each county. The Kendall-side schools are verified. **The Comal-side assignment is unresolved** and is marked UNRESOLVED on the page rather than guessed.

**To resolve:** Address-level confirmation from Boerne ISD and Comal ISD.

### 4. Esperanza WCID #3 tax rate
**Page:** `esperanza.md`
The City of Boerne identifies **WCID #3 (Esperanza)** by name as a customer of its wastewater collection system, so the district's existence is confirmed. **Its current tax rate is not.** A secondary source puts the combined effective rate above 2.50%, which is not verified.

**To resolve:** Pull the district's adopted rate from its own filing or the Kendall County tax office.
**Risk if wrong:** The single largest monthly-cost variable at Esperanza, and the main thing distinguishing it from Regent Park and The Ranches at Creekside on payment.

### 5. Miralomas MUD tax rate
**Page:** `miralomas.md`
Same issue. The Miralomas Municipal Utility District is confirmed to exist and to operate water, wastewater and a reclaimed-water irrigation system. **Its levy is not verified.**

**To resolve:** District filing or Kendall County tax office.

## Tier 2 — Material to a buying decision

### 6. Recurring HOA dues — 38 of 39 communities
**Pages:** all except `anaqua-springs-ranch.md`
Texas management certificates under Tex. Prop. Code § 209.004 are only required to disclose **transfer-related fees**, not recurring assessments. As a result, **no recurring dues figure in this set could be verified from a filed legal document.**

Only Anaqua Springs Ranch publishes its numbers on the association's own site ($1,165/yr dues plus a separate $1,060/yr Gate Sentry fee), and even that is a self-published source rather than a filing.

Secondary dues figures were found for several communities and **deliberately not published**, because they conflict badly. Fallbrook alone produced three incompatible figures from listing sites ($416 semi-annually, $69 monthly, $600 annually).

**To resolve:** One call per association, per management company. This is the highest-volume outstanding item and the one most likely to be filled in incorrectly by a future editor.
**Standing rule for this content set: no dues figure goes on a page without a named primary source and a date.**

### 7. Miralomas county recording discrepancy
**Page:** `miralomas.md`
**Kendall CAD appraises all 268 Miralomas parcels as Kendall County property** (county taxing unit Kendall, school district Boerne ISD), and Bexar County's parcel records return no Miralomas properties. The parcels sit at roughly 29.76 N / 98.80 W, well northwest of the Bexar line.

**But the Miralomas POA management certificate was recorded in the Official Public Records of Bexar County.**

The page states Kendall County (following the appraisal district, which governs the tax bill) and flags the discrepancy.

**To resolve:** Title company review of which recorded instruments actually govern and in which county's records they sit.

### 8. Champee Springs Ranches — is the POA active and what does it assess?
**Page:** `champee-springs-ranches.md`
A POA demonstrably exists — the Champee Springs Ranches Property Owners Association was a named party in litigation decided by the Supreme Court of Texas in 2020. **But no management certificate for it appears in the statewide TREC database**, which § 209.004 would require.

At least one real estate marketing page states the community "does not have an HOA." That claim is inconsistent with the litigation record and should not be repeated.

**To resolve:** Contact the association directly; pull the recorded declaration and any amendments from title; ask specifically about **road maintenance obligations**, which on ranch-scale private roads are often the real recurring cost.
**Also unresolved:** the easement and access litigation history across the original tract is a genuine diligence item for any buyer there.

### 9. Ranger Creek — HOA status
**Page:** `ranger-creek.md`
**No management certificate for a Ranger Creek property owners association appears in the statewide database.** That is evidence of no mandatory HOA, but not proof — it could also indicate non-compliance with the filing requirement or a differently-indexed name.

The page explicitly warns that **recorded deed restrictions run with the land regardless of whether an association exists to enforce them.**

**To resolve:** Title search for recorded restrictions; ask the county about road maintenance responsibility.

### 10. Front Gate — city limits or ETJ?
**Page:** `front-gate.md`
Front Gate is confirmed Bexar County, and its "FG" plat units appear inside the Fair Oaks Ranch master association's unit list. **Whether it sits inside the City of Fair Oaks Ranch corporate limits or in its ETJ is unresolved.** That determines city taxes, city services and permitting.

**Also unresolved:** whether a Front Gate buyer owes assessments to **both** SA Front Gate HOA and FORHA. The page tells readers to ask; it does not assert an answer.

**To resolve:** One call to the City of Fair Oaks Ranch with a specific address.

### 11. Spencer Ranch — the adjacent 112-acre mixed-use component
**Page:** `spencer-ranch.md`
Reporting describes a 112-acre mixed-use component with 560-plus low-rise multifamily units plus retail and office, adjacent to the single-family phase. **Entitlement status, approved density and timing are unverified.**

**To resolve:** City of Boerne planning department — approved development plan and zoning.
**Risk if wrong:** A single-family buyer is unaware of what can be built next door.

### 12. Spencer Ranch — public vs. private streets
**Page:** `spencer-ranch.md`
The filed fee schedule includes a **$525 Road Maintenance Assessment**, which is unusual for a community inside city limits and suggests some streets are privately maintained by the HOA. Unconfirmed.

### 13. Cordillera Ranch — utilities vary by section
**Page:** `cordillera-ranch.md`
Utility service is **not uniform**. Some sections have central water and wastewater; much of the development uses individual on-site septic, with central water available in places. Sourced from the community's own sub-pages, not independently verified, and genuinely variable.

**To resolve:** Section-specific written confirmation for any given property. The page tells readers not to assume one street's arrangement applies to the next.

### 17. Boerne Historic District — Certificate of Appropriateness trigger list
**Page:** `boerne-original-town-historic-district.md`
The City's Historic District Guidelines PDF (the actual rulebook — what triggers review for paint, windows, fencing, signage, additions) is image-based and could not be read. The page describes the process accurately at a high level and tells readers to call Planning.

**Also:** two City of Boerne pages **disagree on the Historic Landmark Commission's meeting schedule** (one says first Tuesday 5:30 p.m., another says second Thursday). The page does not publish a day or time.

**To resolve:** City of Boerne Planning, (830) 248-1501.

### 18. Estancia at Thunder Valley — amenity verification
**Page:** `estancia-at-thunder-valley.md`
The equestrian center, par-3 golf, skeet deck, gym, sport courts and longhorn herd all come from the community's own website — a single self-published source. With only 36 of 87 tracts improved, **which amenities are actually built, open and funded is a real question.** The page flags it and tells readers to ask for the POA budget and reserve position.

### 19. Total acreage and lot counts from marketing vs. record
Several communities market a total footprint larger than what is currently platted and on the appraisal roll — Cordillera Ranch (~8,600 ac marketed vs. 5,444 ac platted), Esperanza (~1,700 ac vs. 571 platted), Miralomas (~900 ac vs. 182 platted), Anaqua Springs Ranch (~920 ac vs. 220 parcels). The pages use the **recorded/appraised** figures and describe the marketed figures as build-out targets. That distinction should be preserved by any future editor.

- **Clearwater Ranch** — two different places share the name. One is in Bexar County at **San Antonio 78255** off Scenic Loop (not a Boerne mailing address at all); the other is a small Kendall County subdivision near Comfort zoned to **Comfort ISD**. Too ambiguous to publish without more work. **Dropped.**
- **Ammann Ranch Estates, Overlook at Boerne, Durango Reserve, Village Park** — real, verified Kendall County subdivisions, but each under 50 parcels with thin supporting data. Held back rather than padded. Candidates for a future batch.

## Standing rules for anyone editing this content set

1. **No HOA dues figure without a named primary source and a date.** Filed management certificates do not contain dues. Listing aggregators are demonstrably unreliable here.
2. **No school assignment without a current district lookup.** Boerne ISD's boundaries changed in August 2024 and the district asks that addresses near boundary lines be confirmed with Administrative Services.
3. **Never infer county from a zip code or a mailing address.** Nine communities in this set are in Bexar County with Boerne mail; two are in Kendall County with a 78015 address.
4. **Never infer county from an HOA management company's address.** FirstService Residential's San Antonio office manages communities in both counties. Miralomas' certificate is recorded in the wrong county entirely.
5. **Appraisal district market values are not asking prices.** Every page says so. Keep it.
