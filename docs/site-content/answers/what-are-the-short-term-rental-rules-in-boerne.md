# What are the short-term rental rules in Boerne, Texas?

Boerne regulates short-term rentals under its own Ordinance No. 2023-09, which covers the city limits and the ETJ. It requires a $200 two-year permit, a safety inspection inside city limits, $500,000 per-occurrence liability insurance, hotel occupancy tax, and a 200-foot separation from another STR on the same block face in residential zones.

## First: Boerne is not San Antonio

This matters more than anything else on this page. **Boerne is the county seat of Kendall County.** It is roughly 31 miles northwest of San Antonio on I-10, but it is a separate incorporated city in a different county. **San Antonio's short-term rental ordinance — its permit types, its fees, its 12.5 percent block-face density cap — has no force in Boerne.** Investors who research "San Antonio STR rules" and then buy in Boerne are reading the wrong rulebook.

Boerne wrote its own. Here is what it says.

## The permit

- **Registration fee: $200.** Renewal fee: **$200**.
- **Permit term: two years** from the effective date.
- **Renewal must be filed within 30 days** of expiration.
- Applies to properties in the **city limits and the extraterritorial jurisdiction (ETJ)**.

Source: [City of Boerne, Short-Term Rentals](https://www.ci.boerne.tx.us/2444/Short-Term-Rentals).

## The safety inspection

An applicant must complete a **safety inspection** of the property. Inside the city limits this is done either by a **city inspector at a $100 fee** or by an outside licensed inspector. **Properties in the ETJ are not required to have the inspection** ([City of Boerne](https://www.ci.boerne.tx.us/2444/Short-Term-Rentals)).

## The insurance requirement

Proof of liability coverage with a minimum of **$500,000 per occurrence and $1,000,000 in the aggregate**. This is not the same as a standard homeowner's policy — most HO-3 policies exclude commercial or transient-occupancy use, and an owner who files an STR permit with a homeowner's declarations page usually finds out the hard way. An **insurance agent** who writes short-term rental policies is the person to ask, before the offer, not after.

## The density rule — the one that kills deals

In areas of the city **zoned for residential use**, a short-term rental **cannot operate within 200 feet of another STR on the same block face**. That includes residentially zoned property inside the Historic, Downtown and River Road overlay districts ([City of Boerne](https://www.ci.boerne.tx.us/CivicAlerts.aspx?AID=1437&ARC=2987)).

In the **commercial** portions of those overlay districts there is no separation requirement. Outside those overlays, **STRs are prohibited in commercial zones**.

The practical consequence: on the streets closest to the Hill Country Mile — exactly the blocks where the nightly rate justifies the purchase — the 200-foot block-face rule can already be consumed by an existing permit holder. **Ask the Planning Department whether the specific address is clear before the option period runs**, not after.

## Occupancy, parking and events

- **Occupancy: two people per bedroom, plus two additional people.**
- **Parking: one space per bedroom.** Street parking is allowed.
- **Events are prohibited.** No weddings, no receptions, no corporate offsites at the property.

These numbers set the ceiling on the revenue model. A three-bedroom in Boerne caps at eight guests, and a listing advertising more than that is advertising a violation.

## Hotel occupancy tax

STR operators owe hotel occupancy tax, remitted through the city portal. In Boerne the combined rate is **13 percent — 6 percent state and 7 percent city** ([City of Boerne, Current Tax Rates](https://www.ci.boerne.tx.us/646/Current-Tax-Rates)).

Whether a booking platform collects and remits any portion of that on the host's behalf is a question for the platform and for a **CPA**. The obligation sits with the operator.

## What about unincorporated Kendall County?

The Boerne ordinance reaches the city limits and the ETJ. **A property in unincorporated Kendall County outside both is not covered by it.** That does not mean there are no rules — deed restrictions, a POA declaration, and county-level requirements can all apply, and Texas counties' authority in this area is narrower than a city's. Confirm the specific parcel with **Kendall County** and read the recorded restrictions before assuming an acreage property is unregulated.

## And the HOA question is separate from all of it

A city permit is permission from the city. It is not permission from a property owners' association. In **Tarr v. Timberwood Park Owners Association** (Tex. 2018) the Texas Supreme Court held that where renters use a single-family home for a residential purpose, the rental does not violate a bare "residential purposes only" covenant regardless of how short the stay. That case is about interpreting **that covenant** — it is not a holding that an HOA can never restrict short-term rentals, and Texas appellate courts have since upheld amended declarations that do restrict them ([Texas Municipal League, Short-Term Rentals](https://www.tml.org/DocumentCenter/View/4951/Short-Term-Rentals---4-2024-PDF)).

Read the declaration. A **Texas real estate attorney** is the right person to tell you what a particular set of restrictions permits.

## Verify before you write the offer

Ordinances change, and this page is current as of **September 2026**. Confirm the terms that apply to a specific address with the **City of Boerne Planning Department at (830) 248-1501** before the option period expires. A permit that cannot be issued is a reason to terminate, but only if you find out in time.
