# What is the Texas homestead exemption in 2026?

The Texas homestead exemption removes part of your home's value from property taxation. For 2026, school districts must exempt $140,000 of a residence homestead's value, up from $100,000 after voters approved Proposition 13 in November 2025. You must own and occupy the home as your principal residence on January 1.

## The number changed, and it changed recently

The mandatory school district residence homestead exemption is **$140,000**. It was **$100,000** before. Texas voters approved **Proposition 13 in November 2025**, and the Legislature implemented it through **SB 4, 89th Legislature**, effective for tax year 2026 ([Texas Comptroller](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/exemptions/)).

That matters because most of what is written about Texas homestead exemptions online is older than the change. A page that says $100,000, or $40,000, or $25,000 is describing a prior version of the law. As of September 2026, the school district figure is $140,000.

The exemption is not a discount on your tax bill. It is a subtraction from the **taxable value** the school district uses. On a home appraised at $500,000 with a homestead exemption, the school district taxes $360,000 instead of $500,000. The dollar savings depend on the school district's rate, which is set locally and changes annually.

## The exemption is not one exemption — it is a stack

A Texas tax bill is not one tax. It is a separate tax from every taxing unit whose boundaries include your property: the school district, the county, the city if you are inside city limits, and any special districts. Each unit applies its own exemptions.

The pieces, per the [Comptroller](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/exemptions/):

- **School district: $140,000 mandatory.** Every school district in Texas must grant it on a qualifying residence homestead.
- **Local option, up to 20% of appraised value.** Any taxing unit may adopt a percentage exemption of up to 20% of appraised value, with a minimum of $5,000. This one is percentage-based, so it grows with your appraisal.
- **County farm-to-market or flood control: $3,000.** A county that collects a farm-to-market road or flood control tax must give a $3,000 residence homestead exemption.
- **Age 65 or older, or disabled: an additional $60,000** from the school district, on top of the $140,000. Other taxing units may adopt a local option of at least $3,000.

Whether your city, county or special district actually adopted a local option exemption is a local decision, and the answer differs by jurisdiction. For a Boerne or Kendall County property, the adopted exemptions by taxing unit are published by the [Kendall Appraisal District](https://kendallad.org/adopted-tax-rates-and-exemptions/).

## Who qualifies

Two conditions, both of them strict:

1. You **owned** the property on **January 1** of the tax year.
2. You **occupied it as your principal residence** on **January 1** of the tax year.

([Texas Comptroller](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/exemptions/))

January 1 is a snapshot date, not a rolling test. A buyer who closes in March does not meet the January 1 test for that year on that property under the general rule, and a seller who moved out in February was still the January 1 owner-occupant. This is a common source of surprise in a first-year tax bill, and it is one of the reasons a newly purchased home's tax bill often looks nothing like the prior owner's.

"Principal residence" means one home. Not a rental, not a second home, not a lot you are holding. One homestead, the one you actually live in.

## The deadline

Applications are due **before May 1** — commonly stated as April 30 ([Texas Comptroller](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/exemptions/)). You file with the **appraisal district** for the county where the property sits, not with the city, not with the school district, and not with the tax assessor-collector who mails the bill.

For a home in Boerne or anywhere else in Kendall County, that is the **Kendall Appraisal District** at [kendallad.org](https://kendallad.org/adopted-tax-rates-and-exemptions/). Not Bexar CAD. Boerne is the county seat of Kendall County and is not part of the City of San Antonio or Bexar County, and a Boerne homeowner who files with Bexar has filed with the wrong district.

There is no fee to file a homestead exemption application with an appraisal district. Companies that mail official-looking letters offering to file it for you for a fee are selling a service you can do yourself.

## What the exemption does besides lower value

Filing a homestead exemption also turns on the **10% appraisal cap**. Once a property has a homestead exemption in place, the appraisal district cannot increase its net appraised value by more than 10% per year, no matter what the market does ([Texas Comptroller](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/exemptions/)).

In a market that has moved the way the Hill Country has, the cap is often worth more over time than the exemption amount itself. A homeowner who has held a homesteaded property through several years of appreciation can be taxed on a value well below what the home would sell for. That gap disappears when the property sells and the cap resets, which is the single most common reason a new owner's tax bill jumps.

## What it does not do

The homestead exemption is a property tax exemption. It is separate from the **Texas homestead protection** in the Property Code and Constitution, which is a creditor-protection concept, and separate from the **Texas home equity rules** that limit borrowing against a homestead. People use the word "homestead" for all three. They are different bodies of law, and a Texas real estate attorney is the right person to sort out which one applies to a specific situation.

It also does not freeze your taxes. The exemption reduces value; the taxing units still set their rates every year, and a rate increase can raise your bill even with the exemption in place and the cap holding.

## What actually goes wrong

- **Never filing.** The exemption is not automatic. It has to be applied for once, and homeowners who moved several years ago and assumed it followed them are a recurring find.
- **Filing with the wrong district.** County lines, not mailing addresses, decide which appraisal district you file with.
- **Losing it in a transfer.** Moving a home into a trust or an LLC, adding or removing an owner, or a death in the family can affect exemption status. The appraisal district decides whether the exemption survives the change, and that question should go to them and to a Texas real estate attorney before the deed is signed, not after.
- **Assuming the prior owner's tax bill is yours.** It reflects their exemptions and their capped value, neither of which transfers to you.

Whether a specific property and a specific owner qualify, and what a given exemption is worth on a given bill, is determined by the appraisal district that appraises the property. For the tax consequences of an ownership change, talk to a CPA.
