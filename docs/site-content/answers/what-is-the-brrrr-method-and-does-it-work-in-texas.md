# What is the BRRRR method, and does it work in Texas?

BRRRR is buy, rehab, rent, refinance, repeat. It works in Texas, with one constitutional catch: if the property is the borrower's homestead, the refinance is a Section 50(a)(6) loan capped at 80 percent loan-to-value with a 12-day waiting period. A rental held as an investment is not the homestead and is not governed by 50(a)(6).

## The five steps

**Buy** a property below its post-repair value, usually one that will not finance conventionally in its current condition. **Rehab** it. **Rent** it, so it produces income and an appraiser has something to value. **Refinance** to pull the invested capital back out. **Repeat** with the recycled capital.

The whole model lives or dies on the fourth step. Everything before it is ordinary renovation work. The refinance is what turns a one-time flip into a repeatable machine, and it is the step where Texas is genuinely different from every other state.

## Why the refinance step is different in Texas

Texas protects the homestead in its **Constitution**, not merely by statute. If the property being refinanced is the borrower's **homestead**, the cash-out is a home equity loan under **Article XVI, Section 50(a)(6)**, and it carries limits that do not exist elsewhere:

- **80 percent loan-to-value maximum.** All loans secured by the homestead, combined, cannot exceed 80 percent of fair market value at closing. At least 20 percent equity must remain.
- **A 12-day waiting period** — the loan cannot close until at least 12 days after the later of the application or the borrower's receipt of the required notice.
- **A 2 percent fee cap** on fees, excluding the appraisal fee, land survey fee, title insurance premium and title search or examination.
- **Generally non-recourse** — the lender cannot pursue the borrower personally for a deficiency.
- **One at a time**, and **12 months** must pass between home equity closings.

Sources: [Rocket Mortgage](https://www.rocketmortgage.com/learn/home-equity-loan-cash-out-refinance-texas), [Hurst Lending](https://hurstlending.com/texas-cash-out-refinance-rules-50a6-guidelines/). Full mechanics: [what is a Texas cash-out refinance under Section 50(a)(6)](/answers/what-is-a-texas-cash-out-refinance-50a6).

For a BRRRR strategy, the two that bite hardest are the 80 percent ceiling and the 12-month rule. The 80 percent ceiling means a fifth of the after-repair value stays trapped in the property permanently. The 12-month rule means a house-hacking investor who refinances their own residence cannot do it again the following quarter, no matter how good the next deal is.

## The investment property is a different animal

**A rental held in the investor's name as an investment property is not the homestead, and is not governed by Section 50(a)(6).** The Texas Constitution's home equity restrictions apply to the homestead. That is the whole reason a classic BRRRR sequence is workable here at all.

The limits that replace them come from the lender, not the constitution: the lender's own loan-to-value requirements, its seasoning requirement — how long the property must be owned or how long the current value must have existed before a cash-out is allowed — its reserve requirements, its debt service coverage test, and its cap on the number of financed properties.

Those vary by lender and by product, and they change. Any specific loan-to-value number or seasoning period on a blog is a number for someone else's loan program. **Get the current terms in writing from a lender before buying the property**, because the whole model is a bet on that refinance existing on those terms four to eight months later.

The failure mode is specific and common: the rehab runs long, the appraisal lands below the projected after-repair value, and the cash-out at the lender's loan-to-value leaves the investor short of the capital they needed to move on. Then the deal that was supposed to recycle capital has consumed it.

## Where the homestead question gets slippery

The clean version is easy: the investor lives in one house and rents four others. The four are investment property.

The version that trips people up is the house-hack. An investor who buys a duplex, lives in one side and rents the other has a homestead. An investor who lives in a property during the rehab and moves out to rent it has to pin down what that property is at the time of the refinance, and whether a homestead designation was ever filed or claimed. **Homestead character is a legal question with a tax consequence attached, and it belongs to a Texas real estate attorney and a CPA, not to an assumption.** Getting it wrong means applying for the wrong loan product and finding out at underwriting.

## The property tax side, which the pro forma usually ignores

Converting a property to a rental changes its property tax treatment in three ways.

**No homestead exemption.** The residence homestead exemptions — including the school district exemption of **$140,000**, raised from $100,000 by Proposition 13, approved by voters in November 2025 and implemented by SB 4 of the 89th Legislature for tax year 2026 — apply only to a **residence homestead** ([Texas Comptroller](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/exemptions/)). A rental gets none of it.

**No 10 percent cap.** The 10 percent annual limit on increases in net appraised value applies to properties that already have a homestead exemption. A rental does not get it.

**The circuit breaker instead — for now.** Non-homestead real property valued at or below an annually indexed threshold is covered by a separate **20 percent** cap on appraised value under **Tax Code Section 23.231**, created by SB 2 (2023) as a pilot for tax years **2024, 2025 and 2026**. It **expires December 31, 2026 unless the Legislature renews it** ([Travis CAD](https://traviscad.org/circuitbreaker), [summary of 2023 Texas property tax law changes](https://resolutepts.com/texas-property-tax-law-changes/)). It excludes land under agricultural special appraisal and homesteads already under the 10 percent cap.

That expiration date is a live underwriting item for anyone buying a Texas rental in 2026 with a five-year hold. A pro forma that assumes 20 percent as a permanent appraisal ceiling is assuming a statute that sunsets. Model it both ways. Detail: [how rental properties are taxed differently in Texas](/answers/how-are-rental-properties-taxed-differently-in-texas).

For Boerne and the rest of Kendall County, the appraisal district is **Kendall Appraisal District (kendallad.org)** — not Bexar CAD. Boerne is in Kendall County, not the City of San Antonio.

## So does BRRRR work in Texas

Yes, with the refinance structured on the investment side rather than against a homestead, and with three things pinned down before the purchase rather than after the rehab:

1. **The lender's actual cash-out terms and seasoning requirement, in writing**, for the product the investor will use.
2. **Whether the property will be the homestead at the time of the refinance**, answered by a Texas real estate attorney and a CPA if there is any ambiguity.
3. **A property tax line that reflects no homestead exemption, no 10 percent cap, and the circuit breaker's December 31, 2026 expiration.**

A model that survives all three is a model. One that assumes national-average refinance terms and homestead tax treatment is a spreadsheet.
