# The Boerne, Texas Real Estate Market

Most market reports about Boerne are wrong before the first paragraph ends, because they treat "Boerne" as one place. It isn't. A Boerne mailing address can put you inside a city with its own electric utility, a dark-sky lighting code and a 1.86 combined tax rate — or seven miles up a county road with a well, a septic system, no zoning of any kind, and a 1.39 rate. Same school district. Same zip. A difference of roughly $2,800 a year in property tax on a median-priced house.

That distinction is the single most important thing to understand about this market, and it drives almost everything below.

## Where the market sits right now

As of September 2026, per SABOR MLS:

| Metric | Figure |
|---|---|
| Active listings | 308 |
| Closed sales, trailing 12 months | 832 |
| Median sale price | ~$602,600 |
| Year-over-year appreciation | +3.0% |

Run the absorption math and you get roughly 69 sales a month against 308 actives — about **4.4 months of inventory**. That is a market that has stopped being a seller's market without becoming a buyer's market. Six months is the traditional balance point; under three months sellers dictate terms. At 4.4 months, Boerne is in the zone where a correctly priced, well-prepared house still sells in a normal window and an overpriced one sits until it gets stale and takes a discount.

The +3.0% year-over-year number is the one people misread. After the 2020-2022 run, 3% feels like a stall. It isn't a stall — it is the market reverting to something close to its long-run behavior. What it does mean is that appreciation is no longer covering your cost of sale. At a 6% total cost to sell, a 3% annual gain means a two-year hold is roughly break-even. Short holds don't work here right now. Plan on five years or don't buy.

## Why the median is $602,600 and what that number hides

A median above $600,000 in a Texas town of about 24,000 people surprises out-of-state buyers. Two things explain it.

First, the land. Kendall County's 2025 certified appraisal roll shows $1.98 billion in agricultural market value inside Boerne ISD alone, appraised at just $9.2 million under productivity valuation — a **$1.97 billion productivity loss** (Kendall Appraisal District, 2025 Certified Totals, as of Supplement 47, December 3, 2025). That is a county where a very large share of the ground is still ranch land carrying an ag valuation. Ranch land converts to residential acreage slowly, and every conversion prices off Hill Country land comps, not subdivision comps.

Second, the mix. Boerne ISD covers 24,924 taxable property accounts with a total market value of $14.74 billion. The City of Boerne accounts for only 9,157 of those accounts. **Roughly two-thirds of the taxable property in this school district sits outside city limits** (Kendall Appraisal District, 2025 Certified Totals). Acreage properties, custom builds on one to ten acres, and gated ranch developments pull the median up in a way a purely municipal market never would.

So "the Boerne market" is really three markets stacked on top of each other: in-town historic and infill, production and semi-custom subdivisions in the growth corridor along I-10, and acreage. They move at different speeds and they do not comp against each other. If somebody hands you a single price-per-square-foot number for Boerne, they are averaging a 1920s cottage on Main Street with a 2,000-foot ranch house on twelve acres. That number is noise.

## The growth driver: people, not speculation

Kendall County's population was estimated at 53,289 in 2025, up 20.3% from 2020 (Community Impact, March 30, 2026, citing Census Bureau estimates). The City of Boerne was estimated at 24,047 as of July 2025. The county consistently ranks among the fastest-growing in Texas by percentage, and the dominant component is domestic in-migration — people moving here, not births.

Domestic in-migration is a more durable demand source than investor speculation, and it behaves differently. It is less rate-sensitive at the margin because the buyer is moving for a reason that isn't financial — a job, a school district, a parent, a retirement plan. It also means your competition as a seller is not just the other listings; it is the buyer's alternative of renting for a year while they figure the area out.

## What actually drives value in Boerne

### 1. Boerne ISD

Boerne ISD earned an **A rating with an overall score of 92** in the Texas Education Agency accountability ratings released in August 2026, covering the 2025-26 school year — the district's seventh consecutive A. Domain scores were 93 in Student Achievement, 83 in School Progress and 90 in Closing the Gaps, with a 99.2% graduation rate (Boerne Star, August 19, 2026).

That rating is the demand engine. It is why a family in a good Bexar County district will still pay a premium to cross the county line. It is also why the western and northern parts of Kendall County — which are served by **Comfort ISD**, not Boerne ISD — price differently. Comfort ISD's 2025 adopted rate was $0.9328 versus Boerne ISD's $1.0109 (Kendall Appraisal District), so the tax is lower, but the school-driven demand premium is not there in the same way. Buyers conflate "Boerne address" with "Boerne ISD" constantly. Verify the district before you write an offer.

### 2. The city-limits line

Adopted rates for tax year 2025, per Kendall Appraisal District:

| Entity | Rate per $100 |
|---|---|
| Boerne ISD | 1.0109 |
| City of Boerne | 0.4716 |
| Kendall County | 0.3770 |
| Cow Creek Groundwater Conservation District | 0.0050 |

Combined, a house inside Boerne city limits in Boerne ISD pays **1.8645**. The same house in unincorporated Kendall County in Boerne ISD pays **1.3929**. On the $602,600 median, that difference is $2,841.86 a year — about $237 a month of escrow, before you touch the loan.

You do not get that for free. Outside the city you are on a well or a shared water system and a septic system, you have no municipal electric or gas, and you have no city inspection backing the house. Inside the city you get Boerne Utilities — the city operates its own electric, natural gas, water, wastewater, stormwater and reclaimed water systems — plus trash, plus a building department. That is a real trade, not a loophole.

### 3. Municipal utility districts

Two MUDs sit inside Kendall County and they are not small additions. Kendall County MUD #1 adds **0.65** to the rate; Miralomas MUD adds **1.00** (Kendall Appraisal District, 2025). A house in Miralomas MUD carries a combined 2.3929 rate — on a $602,600 home with a homestead exemption that is roughly $13,000 a year in property tax versus about $6,978 for the same house in unincorporated Kendall County with no MUD.

MUD disclosure is required in Texas, and it is delivered — but buyers routinely sign it without doing the math. Do the math. A 1.00 MUD rate on a $700,000 house is $7,000 a year, which is roughly $1,100 a month of purchasing power at current rates.

### 4. Water

Kendall County sits in the Hill Country Priority Groundwater Management Area, and the Cow Creek Groundwater Conservation District was created by county voters in November 2002 to manage the Trinity Aquifer (Cow Creek GCD). Outside the city, water is a diligence item on par with the foundation. Well depth, yield, recent drought performance, whether the well is registered, and what the district's drought stage rules do to permitted users are all real questions with real answers. On acreage, "there's a well" is not a finding. Get the yield test.

### 5. No county zoning

The Kendall County Engineer's office states plainly that the county "does not regulate land use and has not adopted zoning regulations in the unincorporated areas of the County," and that the county "has not adopted building codes for single family residential developments" (Kendall County Engineer verification letter, March 4, 2026). The county does permit driveways, work in county right-of-way, outdoor lighting, on-site sewage facilities, floodplain development and site improvements, and it has adopted a Fire Code administered by the Fire Marshal.

Two consequences. One, your protection against what gets built next door is the subdivision's deed restrictions and HOA — nothing else. Read them before you buy, and read them for what they *don't* prohibit. Two, a house built outside the city was not inspected by a municipal building department. Your third-party inspection is doing all of the work. Do not waive it on a country property. Ever.

## Where I think this market goes

I won't forecast prices. Anyone who does is guessing. But three things are structurally true and worth planning around.

Supply is constrained by more than builder appetite. Septic feasibility, well yield, floodplain along Cibolo Creek and its tributaries, and the sheer cost of extending city utilities all cap how fast lots come online. That is a different constraint than a flat metro market with land in every direction.

Demand is driven by in-migration and a school rating, both of which are slow-moving. Neither turns off in a quarter.

And the tax picture is not static. Boerne ISD passed a voter-approval tax rate election on November 4, 2025, canvassed November 18, generating about $4.8 million — $3 million local, $1.8 million state — with more than 80% going to compensation, including raising the teacher minimum starting salary from $55,142 to $60,000 (Community Impact, November 18, 2025). A fast-growth district with rising enrollment will keep coming back to voters. Underwrite your tax escrow with that in mind rather than assuming today's rate is permanent.
