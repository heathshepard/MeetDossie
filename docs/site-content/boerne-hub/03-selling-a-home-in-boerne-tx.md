# Selling a Home in Boerne, TX

Boerne is at roughly **4.4 months of inventory** — 308 active listings against 832 closings over the trailing twelve months, or about 69 sales a month (as of September 2026, per SABOR MLS). Median sale price is about $602,600, up 3.0% year over year.

That combination defines the job. Four and a half months of supply is a market where a correctly priced house sells on a normal timeline and an overpriced one does not sell at all until it takes a visible price cut. And 3% annual appreciation means the market will not bail out a pricing mistake. In 2021 you could list 8% high and let appreciation catch up to you in five months. That is over.

## Pricing: the part that's genuinely hard here

In a tract subdivision with 400 identical floor plans, pricing is arithmetic. In Boerne, on the properties where the money is, it is judgment — and the most common failure I see is an agent running a one-mile radius search and calling it a CMA.

### Why Boerne comps are thin

Three separate markets sit inside the same MLS area and they don't cross-comp:

**In-town Boerne.** Historic district, older infill, walkable to Main Street. Lot value is a large share of total value and condition varies enormously house to house. This is the segment where a bad renovation and a great renovation can sit on the same block with the same square footage and a $150,000 spread.

**Subdivision Boerne.** Production and semi-custom neighborhoods in the growth corridor. This is the closest thing to a normal comp environment, and even here the difference between a 2016 build and a 2024 build in the same subdivision is a real adjustment, not a rounding error.

**Acreage.** Kendall Appraisal District's 2025 certified roll shows $1.98 billion of agricultural market value inside Boerne ISD alone, appraised at $9.2 million under productivity valuation. That is a county where a great deal of the ground is still ranch. When acreage sells, it prices off land comps plus improvement contribution — and improvements on acreage routinely contribute far less than they cost to build.

If your property is acreage, an honest CMA may only have four or five genuinely comparable sales and some of them will be six or twelve months old. That is fine. What is not fine is an agent pretending otherwise and adjusting a subdivision sale up to fit.

### The adjustments that actually move the number in this market

- **Land quality, not just acreage.** Usable, fenced, cleared pasture is worth more per acre than cedar-choked slope. Frontage on a county-maintained road versus a shared gravel easement is a material difference. A live creek or a good stock tank is a premium; a dry drainage that floods is a discount.
- **Water.** A strong, tested, registered well with documented yield is an asset you can put a number on. A well with no records is a discount, because the first buyer's inspection will find whatever is there anyway. Cow Creek Groundwater Conservation District governs groundwater in Kendall County, and buyers are increasingly sophisticated about drought-stage rules.
- **Septic condition and capacity.** A permitted, documented, recently serviced system with capacity matching the bedroom count closes without drama. An undocumented system is a negotiation the buyer will win.
- **School district.** Boerne ISD versus Comfort ISD is a demand difference, and buyers verify it now. If you're on a boundary, get the district's written confirmation before you list so your marketing is accurate and defensible.
- **Tax rate.** In city limits, 1.8645 combined. Unincorporated Boerne ISD, 1.3929. In Kendall County MUD #1, 2.0429. In Miralomas MUD, 2.3929 (Kendall Appraisal District, 2025 adopted rates). Your buyer's lender is qualifying them on that number. A house in a high-MUD district competes against a lower-rate house for a smaller buyer pool at the same price, and pricing has to respect that.

### What your appraisal district value is not

Your Kendall Appraisal District notice is not a market value opinion you can list against. The district's 2025 roll for Boerne ISD shows $14.74 billion in market value against $8.93 billion in net taxable value — a gap created by ag productivity valuation ($1.97 billion), exemptions ($3.24 billion), the 10% homestead appraisal cap ($543 million) and the non-homestead circuit-breaker cap ($68.6 million). If you've owned a while and been protected by the 10% homestead cap, your assessed value may be well below market. If you protested successfully last year, it's lower still. Neither number tells a buyer what your house is worth.

## Prep: what pays and what doesn't

I'll be blunt about this because sellers spend money in the wrong places.

**Pays reliably:**
- Paint, in a neutral. It's the highest-return dollar in the business and it always has been.
- Landscape cleanup and, on acreage, brush clearing along the approach and around the house. The drive in is the first impression and on a rural property it's 90 seconds long.
- Deep clean and declutter. Not staging-magazine empty — just clean.
- Fixing the obvious deferred item the buyer would otherwise use as a lever: the failed water heater, the visibly rotted trim, the fence section on the ground.
- **Documentation.** This is underrated and nearly free. Well records, water test, septic permit and last pump-out, HVAC service history, roof age and any warranty, survey, HOA documents, and — if the land carries an ag valuation — the current ag records. A binder of documents removes the buyer's uncertainty discount. On a country property this is worth more than a new backsplash.

**Usually doesn't pay:**
- Full kitchen remodels immediately before listing. You rarely recover the cost and you're guessing at someone else's taste.
- High-end fixtures in a mid-market house.
- Pools, obviously, but also: outbuildings, shops and arenas built right before sale. They will not appraise at cost.

**The judgment call:** a pre-listing inspection. In a 4.4-month market I usually recommend it on properties over about $700,000 and on anything rural. It costs a few hundred dollars and it converts a surprise during the option period — where the buyer holds all the leverage — into a known item you priced for. The downside is real: in Texas, once you know about a defect you must disclose it. Which means a pre-listing inspection is a commitment to fix or disclose, not a way to find out quietly. I'd rather you make that commitment on purpose than find out in week three.

## Disclosure obligations that bite in Kendall County

The Texas seller's disclosure notice is a legal document and Boerne properties have more boxes that matter than a city house does.

- **Flooding.** Known flooding, floodplain status, and prior claims. Cibolo Creek and its tributaries have flooded the Boerne area repeatedly. Disclose it.
- **Septic.** Type, age, permit status, known issues.
- **Water.** Well, shared well, water supply corporation, MUD, WCID. Shared-well agreements and their terms.
- **Easements and encroachments.** Pipeline, utility, access.
- **HOA and deed restrictions.** Including any active violation.
- **Structural repairs and previous inspections.** Including that pre-listing inspection if you got one.

Because Kendall County has not adopted building codes for single-family residential construction in unincorporated areas (Kendall County Engineer verification letter, March 4, 2026), buyers here lean harder on disclosure and inspection than they would inside a city. An incomplete disclosure is the single most common route from "under contract" to "lawsuit" in this county.

## Timeline

Working backward from a target closing date:

- **2 to 4 weeks before listing**: prep work, documentation gathering, photography scheduling. On acreage, add drone and boundary-overlay imagery — buyers cannot understand a 15-acre tract from ground-level photos.
- **Listing to contract**: in a 4.4-month market, a correctly priced house in a desirable segment typically sees its serious activity in the first two to three weeks. If you've had ten showings and no offer, that's a price signal, not a marketing problem. If you've had two showings in three weeks, that's a price signal too — it means buyers are screening you out before they ever get in the car.
- **Contract to close**: 30 to 45 days financed. Acreage and custom homes appraise slower, and a thin comp set means more appraisal risk. Build that into your expectations.

## Net proceeds: read the whole sheet

A seller net sheet has more lines than most sellers expect. The ones people forget:

- **Commission**, per your listing agreement.
- **Title policy.** In this part of Texas the seller customarily pays the owner's title policy, but it is negotiable and it is negotiated.
- **Escrow, recording, and title company fees.**
- **Survey**, if you're providing one.
- **Prorated property taxes** from January 1 through closing. On a $602,600 house in Boerne city limits with a homestead exemption, the full-year bill is roughly $9,820 — a June closing means you're bringing about half of that to the table. This is the number that surprises sellers most.
- **HOA transfer and resale certificate fees.**
- **Payoff, including per-diem interest** through the funding date, not the contract date.
- **Repair credits or buyer concessions** negotiated during the option period.
- **Ag rollback exposure**, if the land carries a 1-d-1 open-space valuation and the use is changing. This can be substantial and it is worth understanding before you list, not after.

Ask for your net sheet at two or three price points, and ask for it again the day you get an offer. A $15,000 price difference and a $10,000 concession are not the same thing on your bottom line, and they're not the same thing on the buyer's loan either.

## The one thing I'd tell every Boerne seller

Price it right in week one. In a 3%-appreciation market with four and a half months of supply, the price you list at is the price that determines whether you get a first-week showing surge or a slow bleed. Every week a listing sits, the market reads it as a problem, and the eventual sale price after two reductions is almost always lower than the number you would have gotten by starting correctly.
