# Boerne ISD Schools Guide

If you are buying in Boerne for the schools — and a large share of buyers here are — this is the page you need before you tour anything. The district rating is excellent and verifiable. The part that costs people money is the attendance zone, not the rating.

## The current rating, with the actual numbers

In the Texas Education Agency accountability ratings released in August 2026, covering the 2025-26 school year, Boerne ISD earned an **A with an overall score of 92**. The domain breakdown:

| Domain | Score |
|---|---|
| Student Achievement | 93 |
| School Progress | 83 |
| Closing the Gaps | 90 |
| **Overall** | **92 (A)** |

The district also reported a 99.2% graduation rate and college, career and military readiness at 98% (Boerne Star, August 19, 2026). This was the district's seventh consecutive A rating.

In the prior cycle — the 2025 ratings released in August 2025 — Boerne ISD also earned an A, its sixth consecutive, along with 27 distinction designations (Community Impact, August 15, 2025).

Two things worth understanding about what that rating is and isn't:

**School Progress at 83 is the number to watch.** In TEA's system, Student Achievement measures absolute performance and School Progress measures growth. A district with a strong, affluent, well-supported student body tends to score high on achievement almost automatically. Growth is harder. An 83 against a 93 achievement score is the honest read on what the district is doing for students versus what the students bring with them. It is a good score. It is not the same score.

**A district rating is not a campus rating.** TEA rates individual campuses as well as districts, and campus ratings within an A district are not uniform. I am not going to publish individual campus scores here without pulling them directly, and neither should anyone else. Check the current campus rating for the specific school your address feeds into at TxSchools.gov before you make a decision on it. If you want, send me the address and I'll pull it.

## The attendance zone trap — read this twice

This is where Boerne buyers get burned, and it happens several times a year.

**A Boerne mailing address does not mean Boerne ISD.** The western and northern portions of Kendall County are served by **Comfort ISD**, a separate district with its own rating and its own tax rate (0.9328 for 2025 versus Boerne ISD's 1.0109, per Kendall Appraisal District). Properties with Boerne postal addresses fall into Comfort ISD regularly.

**Boerne ISD extends beyond Kendall County.** The City of Fair Oaks Ranch spans Kendall, Comal and Bexar counties, and is served by Boerne ISD and — for Comal County residents — Comal ISD (City of Fair Oaks Ranch). So a Boerne ISD home can be appraised by Kendall Appraisal District, Comal Appraisal District or Bexar Appraisal District, each with a different county tax rate. On a Fair Oaks Ranch street, the school district and the county can both change between one house and the next.

**The district's own guidance is explicit:** "homes located near the school district boundary lines must be verified by the Administrative Services Department." The district provides an InfoFinder tool where you enter a street address and zip code to see assigned schools and bus stops, and Administrative Services can be reached at (830) 357-2011.

Here is my rule, and I don't bend it: **for any address within about a mile of a boundary, I get written confirmation from the district before my client writes an offer.** Not the MLS listing. Not a third-party school-search site. Not the seller's recollection. The district, in writing.

Third-party school sites are wrong often enough that relying on them is negligence. MLS school fields are entered by agents and are not verified by anyone.

## Boundaries move — plan for it

Boerne ISD is a fast-growth district, and fast-growth districts redraw boundaries. The Board of Trustees approved new elementary attendance boundaries in April 2024, creating an attendance zone for Viola Wilson Elementary and providing enrollment relief at Fabra, Kendall and Van Raub Elementary.

The practical implication: if you're buying specifically to attend a particular campus, understand that the assignment is a current condition, not a permanent feature of the property. That's true anywhere, but it's more true in a district adding students every year.

What you can rely on more durably is the district itself. District-level boundaries don't move; campus assignments within them do.

## How schools actually affect home value here

Three mechanisms, in descending order of how much they matter.

### 1. The district premium is real and it's the dominant one

The Boerne ISD rating is the reason families cross the Kendall County line. It shows up as a willingness to pay more and to accept a longer commute. That premium attaches to the district, not to a specific campus, which is why a campus reassignment is less damaging to value than buyers fear.

### 2. Campus-level premiums exist but are smaller and noisier

Within a strong district, campus-to-campus price differences are driven as much by the age and price point of the surrounding housing stock as by the school. A newer subdivision feeding a newer campus prices higher — and it is very hard to separate "the school" from "the houses are newer."

Be skeptical of anyone who tells you a specific elementary is worth a precise dollar premium. The data here is too thin to support that claim honestly.

### 3. The tax that funds it

This runs the other way and buyers underweight it.

Boerne ISD's 2025 adopted rate is **1.0109** per $100. That is the largest single line on a Boerne tax bill — roughly 54% of a combined in-city rate of 1.8645, and 73% of an unincorporated rate of 1.3929 (Kendall Appraisal District, 2025 adopted rates).

The district's rate history tells you something useful:

| Tax year | Boerne ISD rate | School homestead exemption |
|---|---|---|
| 2020 | 1.2519 | $25,000 |
| 2021 | 1.2046 | $25,000 |
| 2022 | 1.1786 | $40,000 |
| 2023 | 0.9932 | $100,000 |
| 2024 | 0.9909 | $100,000 |
| 2025 | 1.0109 | $140,000 |

*(Kendall Appraisal District, Boerne ISD adopted tax rates and exemptions)*

The rate came down substantially from 2020 to 2024 through state compression, and the homestead exemption went from $25,000 to $140,000 over the same span. The 2025 rate ticked back up two cents.

That increase was a **voter-approval tax rate election**. Boerne ISD's 1.0109 rate was adopted contingent on voter approval at a November 4, 2025 election; the district's published rate sheet carried an asterisk reading "Pending Voter Approval — Election Date 11/4/2025" until the result was certified. It passed, and was canvassed November 18, 2025. It generates about $4.8 million — roughly $3 million local plus $1.8 million in state funding — with more than 80% going to compensation, including raising the teacher minimum starting salary from $55,142 to $60,000, auxiliary minimum wage to $15 an hour and paraprofessional minimum to $16 an hour (Community Impact, November 18, 2025).

**Underwrite your escrow accordingly.** A fast-growth district with rising enrollment will keep going back to voters, for both operating rates and bonds. Assuming today's rate is a permanent ceiling is how people end up with an escrow shortage letter in year two.

## Scale, for context

Boerne ISD's 2025 certified appraisal roll covers **24,924 taxable property accounts** with a total market value of **$14.74 billion** and a net taxable value of **$8.93 billion**, producing an approximate levy of **$80.3 million** at the 1.0109 rate. Of those accounts, 11,664 carry a residence homestead exemption and 4,772 over-65 accounts are under a tax ceiling freeze (Kendall Appraisal District, 2025 Certified Totals, as of Supplement 47, December 3, 2025).

The freeze matters for planning. Once a qualifying over-65 homeowner's ceiling is set, further rate or value increases don't raise their school tax. With nearly 4,800 frozen accounts in the district and an aging population, a growing share of the burden of each new rate increase falls on non-frozen accounts — which means younger families with children in those schools.

## The short checklist

Before you make an offer on a house you're buying for Boerne ISD:

1. Confirm the assigned campus with Boerne ISD Administrative Services, in writing.
2. Confirm the county the parcel sits in — especially anywhere near Fair Oaks Ranch.
3. Pull the current campus rating at TxSchools.gov, not the district rating.
4. Get the exact combined tax rate for the parcel, including any MUD.
5. Ask whether the neighborhood was affected by, or is likely to be affected by, a boundary study.
