# Boerne vs. San Antonio: Which Should You Buy In?

The conventional wisdom is that Boerne is the expensive option and San Antonio is the affordable one. On sticker price, that's true — Boerne's median sale price is about $602,600 (as of September 2026, per SABOR MLS), well above the San Antonio metro median.

On the *rate* you pay to own, the conventional wisdom is backwards. Let me show you, with the actual adopted numbers.

## Property taxes: the comparison nobody runs correctly

Most "Boerne is cheaper" and "Boerne is more expensive" claims compare tax *rates* without accounting for exemptions. That's useless, because Bexar County entities offer generous local homestead exemptions and Kendall County entities largely do not. You have to run the math both ways.

### The rates

**Kendall County (tax year 2025, per Kendall Appraisal District):**

| Entity | Rate | Homestead exemption offered |
|---|---|---|
| Boerne ISD | 1.0109 | $140,000 (state-mandated); $60,000 additional for 65+/disabled |
| City of Boerne | 0.4716 | **None** (freeze available for qualifying 65+/disabled) |
| Kendall County | 0.3770 | **None** general; $10,000 for 65+/disabled |
| Cow Creek GCD | 0.0050 | None |
| **Combined, in Boerne city limits** | **1.8645** | |
| **Combined, unincorporated Kendall County** | **1.3929** | |

**Bexar County (tax year 2025, per Bexar County official rates):**

| Entity | Rate | Homestead exemption offered |
|---|---|---|
| Northside ISD | 1.004900 | $140,000 (state-mandated) |
| City of San Antonio | 0.541590 | $5,000 or 20% |
| Bexar County | 0.276331 | $5,000 or 20% |
| Hospital District (University Health) | 0.276235 | $5,000 or 20% |
| Alamo Community College District | 0.149150 | $5,000 or 1% |
| Bexar County Road and Flood Control | 0.023668 | $3,000 and 20% |
| San Antonio River Authority | 0.018300 | $5,000 or 4% |
| **Combined** | **2.290174** | |

Six county-wide entities stack in Bexar County before you even reach the school district. Kendall County has effectively two, plus a groundwater district that rounds to nothing.

### The math on an identical $602,600 house

Same price, homestead exemption filed, 2025 adopted rates:

| Location | Annual property tax | Effective rate |
|---|---|---|
| Unincorporated Kendall County, Boerne ISD | **$6,978** | 1.16% |
| Inside Boerne city limits, Boerne ISD | **$9,820** | 1.63% |
| Inside San Antonio city limits, Northside ISD | **$11,032** | 1.83% |

*Assumptions: $602,600 assessed value, general residence homestead only (no over-65, disability or veteran exemption), 2025 adopted rates as published by Kendall Appraisal District and Bexar County. A Bexar County property in Alamo Heights ISD or San Antonio ISD, or in unincorporated Bexar County, produces a different number. Your actual bill depends on your parcel.*

So on the same purchase price, Boerne inside the city limits runs about **$1,200 a year cheaper** than San Antonio, and unincorporated Kendall County runs about **$4,050 a year cheaper** — roughly $338 a month of mortgage payment.

That is real and it surprises people. It survives even though San Antonio and Bexar County each give a 20% local homestead exemption that Boerne and Kendall County do not, because San Antonio simply has more entities taxing the same dirt.

### The honest caveat

This compares the same price point, and that is not how most people actually shop. If your budget is $400,000, San Antonio has hundreds of options and Boerne has very few. The Boerne buyer is usually spending more in absolute dollars even though the rate is lower — they are buying a larger or newer house on more land, and the lower rate applied to a higher value can still produce a bigger check.

Run the comparison on the two houses you would actually buy, not on a hypothetical identical one.

### The in-between case: Fair Oaks Ranch

There is a third option people miss. The City of Fair Oaks Ranch spans three counties — Kendall, Comal and Bexar — and property there is appraised by Kendall Appraisal District, Comal Appraisal District or Bexar Appraisal District depending on which side of the line your parcel falls on. The city is served by Boerne ISD and, for Comal County residents, Comal ISD. City property taxes are billed by the Bexar County Tax Assessor-Collector regardless of which county you live in (City of Fair Oaks Ranch).

The City of Fair Oaks Ranch's adopted 2025 rate is **0.285300** with a $5,000-or-percentage homestead exemption and a $50,000 over-65 exemption (Bexar County 2025 Official Tax Rates). That's a materially lower city rate than either Boerne (0.4716, no homestead exemption) or San Antonio (0.541590).

This is the single most error-prone address in the region. Two houses on the same Fair Oaks Ranch street can sit in different counties, be appraised by different appraisal districts, and land in different school districts. Do not assume. Pull the parcel.

## Schools

Boerne ISD earned an **A rating with an overall score of 92** in the TEA accountability ratings released in August 2026, covering the 2025-26 school year — the district's seventh consecutive A, with a 99.2% graduation rate (Boerne Star, August 19, 2026).

That's the cleanest single-variable advantage Boerne has. Bexar County contains excellent schools — Alamo Heights ISD, parts of Northside and North East ISD all perform strongly — but it also contains some of the state's weakest, and the distribution matters. In Bexar County you buy into a specific attendance zone and you monitor it, because zones get redrawn and campus performance varies within a district. In Boerne ISD, you're buying into a district that has rated A district-wide for seven straight years.

Two caveats a good agent gives you anyway:

**Not every Boerne address is Boerne ISD.** Western and northern Kendall County is Comfort ISD. Verify with the district, not the listing.

**Boerne ISD is a fast-growth district and boundaries move.** The board approved new elementary attendance boundaries in April 2024, creating a zone for Viola Wilson Elementary and relieving enrollment at Fabra, Kendall and Van Raub. If you're buying specifically for a campus, understand that the campus assignment is not permanent.

## Commute

The corridor is I-10, and it's the whole conversation.

If you work in the northwest quadrant — the Medical Center, La Cantera, the UTSA area, the 1604/I-10 employment corridor — the Boerne commute is manageable and thousands of people do it daily. If you work downtown, on the South Side, or east of US 281, you are adding meaningful time in both directions and you should drive it at 7:15 a.m. on a Tuesday before you commit to it. Not on a Sunday afternoon. A Tuesday.

The chokepoint has historically been where Kendall County traffic meets Bexar County volume approaching Loop 1604. TxDOT has worked this corridor in stages, with a series of projects between Loop 1604 and SH 46 improving intersections, adding and widening overpasses, converting frontage roads to one-way operation and expanding main lanes. A further project covering I-10 from SH 46 to Fair Oaks Parkway in Kendall County has been studied at a proposed cost of $100 million (TxDOT, San Antonio District). Construction on a corridor like this is a decade-long condition, not an event — factor it in.

The upside nobody mentions: the commute is directional. Boerne to San Antonio in the morning and back in the evening runs with the flow, which is different from a suburb where you're fighting everyone.

## Cost of living beyond the tax bill

**Utilities.** Inside Boerne city limits, the City owns and operates six utilities — electric, natural gas, water, wastewater, stormwater and reclaimed water. That means one provider, one bill, and rates set by City Council rather than by a competitive retail market. In February 2025 the average residential water and wastewater bill went from $90.46 to $94.71 per month, and the city has signaled further increases over a five-year horizon to fund its capital improvement plan (Community Impact, February 3, 2025). Outside city limits, electric service comes from a cooperative — Bandera Electric Cooperative and Pedernales Electric Cooperative both serve parts of Kendall County — and water and sewer are your problem: a well and a septic system, with the capital cost and maintenance that implies.

In San Antonio you get CPS Energy and SAWS, both large municipally-owned utilities with their own rate trajectories.

**Sales tax** is 8.25% in Boerne — 6.25% state, 1.50% city, 0.50% county (City of Boerne). That's the Texas cap and it matches San Antonio.

**Insurance.** Both markets sit in a Texas homeowners insurance environment that runs well above the national average, and hail is the driving peril in this part of the state. Get an actual quote on the actual address before you commit — see the [cost of living guide](/boerne/cost-of-living) for more on that.

**Everything else.** Boerne has a real grocery, restaurant and services base for a town its size, but it does not have San Antonio's depth. Specialty medical care, major-airport access, professional sports, and the long tail of retail all live in San Antonio. If you're in Boerne, you drive down for those. That's fine — it's an hour of your Saturday — but it's a genuine cost that doesn't show up on a spreadsheet.

## Lifestyle and land use

This is where the two are least alike, and it's less about "small town versus city" than about how the land is governed.

**Kendall County does not have zoning.** The County Engineer's office states that the county "does not regulate land use and has not adopted zoning regulations in the unincorporated areas of the County," and that it "has not adopted building codes for single family residential developments" (Kendall County Engineer verification letter, March 4, 2026). The county does permit driveways, work in county right-of-way, outdoor lighting, on-site sewage facilities, floodplain development and site improvements, and has adopted a Fire Code administered by the Fire Marshal.

Cut both ways:
- **For you**: you can build a shop, keep animals, run a business from a barn, and generally use your land without asking permission from a planning commission.
- **Against you**: so can your neighbor. Your only protection is the subdivision's deed restrictions and HOA. Read them before you buy, and read them for what they permit as much as what they forbid.

Inside Boerne city limits, you get the opposite: a Unified Development Code, zoning, a building department, and a dark-sky lighting ordinance. Predictability, at the cost of flexibility.

San Antonio, for all its size, is a zoned city with a code department. If you want to know what can be built next door, there's an answer. In unincorporated Kendall County, often there isn't one.

## Where San Antonio genuinely wins

I sell in both markets and I'd be doing you a disservice if I only argued one side.

**Budget under about $450,000.** Boerne's inventory at that price is thin and getting thinner. San Antonio has real choice.

**A downtown, South Side or east-side job.** The commute math stops working.

**You want walkable urban.** Boerne's downtown is genuinely charming and genuinely small. Southtown, Alamo Heights, Olmos Park and King William offer an urban life Boerne does not.

**You need frequent specialty medical care.** Proximity to the Medical Center is worth more than a lower tax rate.

**You want maximum liquidity.** San Antonio's deeper buyer pool means a faster, more predictable resale, particularly in the mid-market. A $1.4 million acreage property in Kendall County has a small buyer pool and can take a long time to sell.

**You're a short-term investor.** At 3% annual appreciation and a 6% cost of sale, neither market rewards a two-year hold, but Boerne's thinner resale market makes a fast exit harder.

## The short version

Buy Boerne if you want the school district, the land, the lower effective tax rate, and a small-town base — and you're planning to be here five or more years. Buy San Antonio if your budget, your job location, or your need for urban density says so. Both are defensible. What isn't defensible is picking based on a rate comparison somebody ran without the exemptions in it.
