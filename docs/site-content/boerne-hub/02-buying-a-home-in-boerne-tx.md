# Buying a Home in Boerne, TX

The mechanics of a Texas purchase are the same in Boerne as anywhere else in the state: TREC contract, option period, third-party financing addendum, title commitment, survey, closing at a title company. If you've bought in Texas before, none of that will surprise you.

What's different is the diligence. In San Antonio, the systems question is "does the AC work." In Kendall County, outside the city, the systems question includes "where does the water come from, where does the sewage go, and who inspected any of this." That is where Boerne purchases go wrong, and it is almost always a diligence failure rather than a negotiation failure.

## Step one, before you tour anything: figure out which Boerne you're buying

Do this before you fall in love with a listing. There are four questions and they change the price of ownership by thousands a year.

**1. Is it inside Boerne city limits?**
Inside, the combined 2025 tax rate for a Boerne ISD property is 1.8645 per $100. Outside, in unincorporated Kendall County, it's 1.3929 (Kendall Appraisal District, 2025 adopted rates). On a $600,000 house that is about $2,830 a year. Inside the city you also get Boerne Utilities — city electric, natural gas, water, wastewater, stormwater and reclaimed water — and a building department. Outside you get well, septic, and an electric cooperative.

**2. Is it in a MUD?**
Kendall County MUD #1 adds 0.65 to the rate. Miralomas MUD adds 1.00. That is not a rounding error — a 1.00 MUD rate on a $700,000 home is $7,000 a year. Texas requires MUD notice, and you will get it, but get it early enough to matter rather than on the day you sign.

**3. Is it Boerne ISD or Comfort ISD?**
A Boerne mailing address does not guarantee Boerne ISD. The western and northern reaches of Kendall County are Comfort ISD. Boerne ISD's own guidance is that "homes located near the school district boundary lines must be verified by the Administrative Services Department" — the district's InfoFinder tool plus a phone call to Administrative Services at (830) 357-2011 is the only answer I trust. Not the listing. Not Zillow. Not the seller.

**4. Is there an HOA, and what do its restrictions actually say?**
This matters more in Kendall County than it does in a city, because outside the municipal limits the deed restrictions are the *only* land-use control. Kendall County's own County Engineer states the county "does not regulate land use and has not adopted zoning regulations in the unincorporated areas" (Kendall County Engineer verification letter, March 4, 2026). If the restrictions don't prohibit it, it can be built next to you.

## The country-property diligence list

This is the part that separates a Boerne buyer's agent from someone who works the whole metro and lands here twice a year.

### Water

If it's a well, you need three things: a yield test, a bacteriological and chemical water test, and the well's registration status with the Cow Creek Groundwater Conservation District. Kendall County sits inside the Hill Country Priority Groundwater Management Area, and Cow Creek GCD was created by county voters in 2002 specifically to manage Trinity Aquifer withdrawal.

The failure mode is a well that produces fine in March and struggles in August. Ask the seller for their pump records and ask the neighbors what happened in the last drought — neighbors are a better data source than any document on this particular question. If it's a shared well, the shared-well agreement needs to be read line by line: who pays for the pump, what happens when it fails, and what the remedy is if the other party won't pay.

If it's a water supply corporation or a WCID, get the connection fee and the monthly rate in writing before the option period ends.

### Septic (OSSF)

Kendall County permits on-site sewage facilities, and the county's permitting authority approves or denies an application within 30 days of receipt (Kendall County). For an existing system, you want the permit on file, the design, the last pump-out record, and a dedicated septic inspection — not the general home inspector's five-minute look. Aerobic systems have maintenance contracts that transfer or lapse at closing; find out which.

For vacant land, the entire deal hinges on whether a system can be permitted at all. Soil, slope and setbacks decide that, and the answer requires a licensed site evaluator. Make the contract contingent on it.

### The house itself

Kendall County "has not adopted building codes for single family residential developments" (County Engineer, March 2026). The county has adopted a Fire Code, administered by the Fire Marshal, and issues Certificates of Compliance and Occupancy for certain multi-family and non-residential development — but a single-family house built outside the city was not inspected by a municipal building department.

Your third-party inspection is the entire quality-control system on that house. Use a good inspector, be present, and budget for specialists: foundation, roof, septic, well, and — on older Hill Country homes — a look at the electrical service if there's any knob-and-tube or an early Federal Pacific panel.

### Floodplain and drainage

Cibolo Creek and its tributaries have produced significant flooding in the Boerne area repeatedly. Flood zone designation varies parcel by parcel, sometimes within the same subdivision, and the FEMA FIRM panel for the exact parcel is the only reliable source. Texas seller's disclosure requires disclosure of known flooding and certain floodplain status, and I read that section carefully on every Boerne contract.

Two additional notes. FEMA maps do not capture every risk in Central Texas — flash flooding happens outside mapped zones routinely. And on acreage, ask where the low-water crossing is between the house and the highway. A property that is dry but unreachable eight times a year is a real problem that no map will tell you about.

### Access and easements

On rural tracts, confirm legal access, not just a gravel road that has always been there. The survey and the title commitment's Schedule B are where this lives. Pipeline easements, utility easements and shared driveway arrangements all show up here. Order a new survey — an old one that predates a fence line, a pond or an outbuilding will cost you more in a title fight than the survey saved.

### Outdoor lighting

Kendall County requires a permit prior to installing any outdoor lights, and regulated outdoor lighting must use International Dark-Sky Association compliant products. The City of Boerne has its own dark-sky regulations in its Unified Development Code. If you're planning arena lights, a lit sport court or extensive landscape lighting, check before you budget for it.

## Timeline

A typical financed Boerne purchase runs 30 to 45 days from executed contract to funding. The pieces:

- **Option period**: negotiated, commonly 7 to 10 days. On a country property I push for longer, because you are stacking a general inspection, a septic inspection, a well test and sometimes a structural engineer inside that window, and rural inspectors book out further than city ones.
- **Earnest money and option fee**: delivered per the contract's timelines. Texas contract deadlines are strict and the clock does not care about weekends or holidays.
- **Survey**: order it the day the option period starts, not the day before closing. Rural surveys take longer.
- **Appraisal**: acreage and custom homes appraise slower and with more variance, because the comparable set is thin. An appraiser working from a San Antonio comp pool will get an acreage property wrong. Ask your lender to order a local appraiser.
- **Title**: Schedule B review is a real step here, not a formality.

One timing note that costs people money: if your contract executes on a Wednesday, Thursday or Friday, your option period is running while the title company is closed for part of it. Under a short option, that can leave you delivering an option fee or terminating on a day when nobody is available to help. Push execution to earlier in the week when you can, or negotiate a longer option.

## Financing considerations specific to this market

**Acreage and the appraisal.** Many conventional products limit how much of a property's value can be in land versus improvements, and outbuildings, barns and arenas often contribute far less to appraised value than they cost to build. If you're buying a property where half the value is a shop and twelve acres, talk to a lender who does rural lending in this county before you write.

**Ag valuation.** If the land you're buying carries a 1-d-1 open-space agricultural valuation and you don't continue a qualifying use, you can trigger a rollback. The Kendall Appraisal District's ag guidelines are specific — cow/calf requires a minimum of three calves on ten acres, beekeeping requires not less than five nor more than twenty acres with a minimum of six hives, wildlife management requires a minimum of three approved practices, and the land must have been in qualifying ag use for five of the preceding seven years (Kendall County Ag Use Manual). Decide before closing whether you intend to maintain the valuation.

**Homestead exemption.** File it. Texas Tax Code 11.13(b) requires school districts to provide a $140,000 residence homestead exemption, and Kendall Appraisal District's adopted exemption schedule shows that $140,000 applied at Boerne ISD and Comfort ISD. Note carefully: **neither the City of Boerne nor Kendall County offers a general residence homestead exemption** (Kendall Appraisal District adopted rates and exemptions). Kendall County offers $10,000 for over-65 and disabled persons; the City of Boerne offers a freeze for qualifying over-65 and disabled homesteads but no general homestead percentage. So the full value of your homestead exemption here is the school-district piece — roughly $1,415 a year at the 2025 Boerne ISD rate.

Also worth knowing: a buyer who acquires property after January 1 may receive the residence homestead exemption for the applicable portion of that tax year immediately upon qualifying, if the prior owner did not already have it for that year (Kendall Appraisal District, Updated Property Tax Information, March 2026).

## Short-term rentals — do not import San Antonio's rules

If you're buying with any intent to short-term rent, **the City of San Antonio's STR ordinance does not apply in Boerne.** Boerne is in Kendall County and is its own municipality with its own rules.

Under the City of Boerne's short-term rental ordinance, an STR operating inside city limits or in the city's extraterritorial jurisdiction must be registered and permitted by the City. The permit fee is $200, permits expire two years from the effective date with a $200 renewal, and properties inside city limits require a safety inspection at a cost of $100 (ETJ properties are exempt from the inspection requirement). In residentially zoned areas, an STR cannot operate within 200 feet of another STR on the same block face. Owners must carry minimum liability coverage of $500,000 per occurrence and $1 million in the aggregate, occupancy is limited to two people per bedroom plus two additional, and hotel occupancy taxes must be remitted. The City's Planning Department is at (830) 248-1501.

For property in unincorporated Kendall County outside any city's ETJ, **verify the rules directly with Kendall County and the City of Boerne before you underwrite an STR.** I am not going to tell you it's unregulated based on the absence of an ordinance I found online. Call and get it in writing.
