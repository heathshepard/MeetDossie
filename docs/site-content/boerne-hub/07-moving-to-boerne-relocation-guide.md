# Moving to Boerne, TX: A Relocation Guide

Kendall County's population was estimated at 53,289 in 2025, up **20.3% since 2020**, with domestic in-migration — people moving here from elsewhere in the U.S. — as the dominant driver (Community Impact, March 30, 2026, citing Census Bureau estimates). The City of Boerne was estimated at 24,047 as of July 2025.

You're not the first person to have this idea. You are, however, probably about to make one of about five specific mistakes that out-of-state buyers make in this county. Let's go through them.

## Mistake 1: assuming the seller's tax bill is your tax bill

This is the expensive one, and it catches nearly every relocation buyer.

Texas has no state income tax. It funds local government through property tax, and property is appraised at market value annually. There are two protections, and **neither one transfers to you**:

**The 10% homestead appraisal cap.** Once you have a homestead exemption in place, your appraised value can rise no more than 10% per year plus the value of new improvements. A homeowner who bought in 2015 may be assessed far below market today. In Boerne ISD alone, that cap removed **$543 million** of value from the 2025 roll (Kendall Appraisal District, 2025 Certified Totals).

**The over-65 tax ceiling freeze.** Kendall County, the City of Boerne, Boerne ISD and Comfort ISD all offer a tax freeze for qualifying over-65 or disabled homesteads. Boerne ISD's 2025 roll shows **4,772 over-65 accounts under a ceiling** (Kendall Appraisal District).

When you buy, the cap resets and the freeze ends. The property gets reappraised to market — which, conveniently for the appraisal district, is the price you just paid. If you're buying from a long-tenured owner or a senior under a freeze, **your first full-year tax bill can be dramatically higher than the tax figure on the MLS listing.**

Never, ever underwrite off the current tax bill. Underwrite off your purchase price times the parcel's combined rate, minus the $140,000 school homestead exemption. I run that number for every buyer before they write an offer, and I recommend you make any agent you work with do the same.

### For California buyers specifically

Your instinct from Proposition 13 is that acquisition value locks in and grows slowly. Texas is the opposite: annual reappraisal at market, with a 10% cap that only applies once you have a homestead exemption and only to a homestead. There is no acquisition-value lock.

The offset is that Texas has no state income tax, and for a high earner that is frequently a larger number than the property tax difference. Run both, together, with your CPA. Do not run either one alone.

### For Austin buyers

You already understand Texas property tax. What's different is the exemption structure. **Neither the City of Boerne nor Kendall County offers a general residence homestead exemption** (Kendall Appraisal District). Your only general homestead exemption here is the $140,000 school district exemption. Check what your current taxing entities offer before you assume it's a wash.

## Mistake 2: assuming a Boerne address means Boerne ISD and Kendall County

Three separate traps live here, and all three are common.

**Comfort ISD.** The western and northern parts of Kendall County are served by Comfort ISD, not Boerne ISD — different rating, different rate (0.9328 vs. 1.0109 for 2025). Plenty of Comfort ISD homes have Boerne mailing addresses.

**Fair Oaks Ranch.** The City of Fair Oaks Ranch spans Kendall, Comal and Bexar counties. Property is appraised by Kendall Appraisal District, Comal Appraisal District or Bexar Appraisal District depending on the parcel, and the city is served by Boerne ISD and, for Comal County residents, Comal ISD. City property taxes are billed by the Bexar County Tax Assessor-Collector regardless of which county you live in (City of Fair Oaks Ranch). Two houses on the same street can be in different counties and different school districts.

**Boundary proximity.** Boerne ISD's own guidance is that homes near district boundary lines "must be verified by the Administrative Services Department." Get it in writing. The district can be reached at (830) 357-2011.

Boerne ISD earned an A with an overall score of 92 in the TEA ratings released in August 2026 — its seventh consecutive A, with a 99.2% graduation rate (Boerne Star, August 19, 2026). That rating is a big part of why you're reading this page. Make sure you're actually buying into it.

## Mistake 3: buying rural without understanding what rural means here

Roughly two-thirds of the taxable property in Boerne ISD sits outside Boerne city limits — the district covers 24,924 accounts while the city covers 9,157 (Kendall Appraisal District, 2025 Certified Totals). If you're picturing land, you're picturing unincorporated Kendall County. Here's what that actually entails.

**Water is a well.** Kendall County sits in the Hill Country Priority Groundwater Management Area over the Trinity Aquifer, and the Cow Creek Groundwater Conservation District was created by county voters in 2002 to manage it. A well needs a yield test, a water quality test, registration status confirmed, and — honestly — a conversation with the neighbors about how it performed in the last serious drought. A well that's fine in March and marginal in August is the classic failure mode.

**Sewer is a septic system.** Kendall County permits on-site sewage facilities and approves or denies applications within 30 days of receipt. For an existing house, get the permit, the design, the service history, and a dedicated septic inspection — not the general inspector's quick look. For vacant land, the whole deal depends on whether a system can be permitted, and only a licensed site evaluator can tell you.

**Nobody inspected the house.** The Kendall County Engineer's office states the county "has not adopted building codes for single family residential developments" in the unincorporated areas. The county does permit driveways, work in county right-of-way, outdoor lighting, floodplain development, on-site sewage facilities and site improvements, and it has adopted a Fire Code administered by the Fire Marshal — but a single-family house built in the county was not inspected by a municipal building department. Your third-party inspection is the entire quality-control system. Budget for specialists.

**There is no zoning.** The same letter states the county "does not regulate land use and has not adopted zoning regulations in the unincorporated areas of the County." Your only protection against what happens next door is the subdivision's deed restrictions and HOA. Read them before you buy — for what they permit as well as what they forbid.

**Outdoor lighting is regulated.** Kendall County requires a permit before installing any outdoor lights and requires International Dark-Sky Association compliant products. The City of Boerne has its own dark-sky regulations in its Unified Development Code. This is a genuinely nice feature of living here — the night sky is worth the trouble — but check before you budget for lighting.

## Mistake 4: underbudgeting insurance

Texas homeowners insurance runs well above the national average, and hail is the driving reason. Roof age and material will drive your premium and can affect whether you're written at replacement cost or actual cash value. Rural properties rate worse on distance-to-fire-station. Wooded acreage draws wildfire underwriting scrutiny.

Get a **bound quote on the specific address** during your option period, from an agent who writes in Kendall County. Not a range, not an online estimate — a real quote on that roof at that address. I've seen quotes on similar-looking properties come back thousands apart, and insurance is one of the few surprises that can break a deal on debt-to-income at the last minute.

## Mistake 5: not driving the commute

If you'll be commuting into San Antonio, drive it on a Tuesday at 7:15 a.m. before you commit. The corridor is I-10, TxDOT has been reworking it in stages for years, and the experience differs enormously depending on whether you're headed to the northwest employment corridor or downtown. A commute that's pleasant on a Sunday afternoon can be a different life at 7:15 on a weekday.

## The timeline that actually works

**Three to six months out**
- Get pre-approved with a lender who does rural lending in Kendall County. Acreage, outbuildings and thin comp sets create appraisal and product issues that a generic online lender will discover too late.
- Decide the fundamental question: in town or on land. They are different purchases with different diligence, different costs, and different resale profiles.
- Pull tax estimates at your target price for the jurisdictions you're considering.

**Two to three months out**
- Come for a scouting trip. Drive the commute. Drive it at rush hour.
- Tour in-town and acreage on the same trip so you can feel the difference rather than theorize about it.
- If schools matter, visit the campuses and verify the zones with the district.

**Under contract**
- Order the survey the day the option period starts.
- Schedule inspection, septic inspection and well testing immediately. Rural inspectors book further out than city ones — negotiate a longer option period on a country property for exactly this reason.
- Get the bound insurance quote.
- Read the HOA documents and the title commitment's Schedule B in full.
- One scheduling note that costs people money: if your contract executes Wednesday through Friday, part of your option period runs while the title company is closed. Under a short option that's a real problem. Push execution earlier in the week when you can.

**After closing**
- **File your homestead exemption.** The general deadline for filing an exemption application is before May 1 (Texas Comptroller). A buyer who acquires property after January 1 may receive the residence homestead exemption for the applicable portion of that tax year immediately upon qualifying, if the prior owner didn't already have it for that year (Kendall Appraisal District, March 2026). Filing is free — do it yourself and ignore anyone who mails you an offer to file it for a fee.
- Put **May 15** on your calendar for protest season. See the [property tax guide](/boerne/property-taxes) for how that works.
- Register your well with Cow Creek GCD if it isn't already.

## What I'd want you to know that isn't on a spreadsheet

Boerne is a small town that is growing fast, and it feels like both of those things at once. Downtown is genuinely walkable and genuinely charming, and it is also genuinely small. People are friendly in a way that surprises coastal transplants and they are also aware that a lot of new people are arriving. Showing up curious goes a long way.

The Hill Country is beautiful and it is hard country. It floods fast, it droughts hard, cedar pollen in winter is real, and the deer will eat every plant you own. None of that is a reason not to come. It is a reason to buy with your eyes open rather than off a photograph.
