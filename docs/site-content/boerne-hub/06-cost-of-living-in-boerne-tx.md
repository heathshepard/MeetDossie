# Cost of Living in Boerne, TX

Most cost-of-living calculators are useless for Boerne, because they index against a city average and Boerne isn't one place. The single biggest variable in your monthly cost of ownership is whether your parcel sits inside Boerne city limits, in unincorporated Kendall County, or inside a municipal utility district. That one fact can swing your housing cost by several hundred dollars a month on an identical house.

Here's what I can document.

## Property tax: the biggest line

### The exemption fact almost nobody tells you

**Neither the City of Boerne nor Kendall County offers a general residence homestead exemption.** Kendall County offers $10,000 for over-65 and disabled persons. The City of Boerne offers a tax ceiling freeze for qualifying over-65 and disabled homesteads but no general homestead reduction (Kendall Appraisal District, adopted tax rates and exemptions).

The only general homestead exemption on a Boerne tax bill is the state-mandated school district exemption of **$140,000**, which appears at both Boerne ISD and Comfort ISD on the district's published exemption schedule.

That is meaningfully less generous than Bexar County, where the county, the city, the hospital district, the community college district and the river authority each offer their own local homestead exemption. If you're moving from San Antonio, do not assume your exemption savings will scale.

### What that means in dollars

On the median Boerne-area sale price of about $602,600 (as of September 2026, per SABOR MLS), with a general residence homestead exemption:

| Location | Annual tax | Monthly escrow |
|---|---|---|
| Unincorporated Kendall County, Boerne ISD | **$6,978** | ~$582 |
| Inside Boerne city limits, Boerne ISD | **$9,820** | ~$818 |
| Unincorporated, Kendall County MUD #1 | **$10,895** | ~$908 |
| Unincorporated, Miralomas MUD | **$13,004** | ~$1,084 |
| Unincorporated Kendall County, Comfort ISD | **$6,617** | ~$551 |

*Calculated from 2025 adopted rates published by Kendall Appraisal District, with the $140,000 school homestead exemption and no other exemptions applied.*

The homestead exemption itself is worth about **$1,415 a year** at the Boerne ISD rate — meaningful, but not the multi-thousand-dollar reduction people expect. File it anyway; it also starts your 10% annual appraisal cap.

## Sales tax

**8.25%** in Boerne: 6.25% state, 1.50% city, 0.50% county (City of Boerne). That's the Texas statutory maximum and it matches San Antonio. No advantage either direction.

Boerne's hotel occupancy tax is 7% city on top of 6% state — relevant if you're operating a short-term rental, irrelevant if you're not.

## Utilities

### Inside city limits

The City of Boerne owns and operates **six utilities**: electric, natural gas, water, wastewater, stormwater and reclaimed water. One provider, one bill, rates set by City Council.

What I can document on cost: in February 2025, the average residential water-and-wastewater bill moved from **$90.46 to $94.71 per month**. The base charge rose about 7% — a 5/8-inch connection went from $28.11 to $30.08, and a 3/4-inch connection from $58.58 to $62.68. The City has indicated further increases over a five-year horizon to fund its Boerne Utilities Capital Improvement Plan (Community Impact, February 3, 2025).

Note the meter-size detail, because it matters and buyers miss it. A 3/4-inch meter carries roughly double the base charge of a 5/8-inch meter before you use a single gallon. Larger and newer homes frequently have larger meters. Ask what meter is on the house.

Electric and gas rates vary by customer classification. Get the actual twelve-month utility history from the seller during your option period — it is a far better predictor than any average, and on a house with a pool, a well pump or a detached shop it will be substantially higher than the city average.

### Outside city limits

No city utilities. Electric service comes from a cooperative — **Bandera Electric Cooperative** and **Pedernales Electric Cooperative** both serve portions of Kendall County. The City's own electric service area is defined by the Public Utility Commission and covers most of the city limits plus some areas beyond it, so the boundary is not simply the city line. Verify who serves the specific address.

Water is a well or a water supply corporation. Sewer is a septic system. Propane replaces natural gas in most rural locations.

**Budget for rural infrastructure as a capital item, not a utility bill:**
- Well pump replacement is a real, periodic expense and it happens without warning.
- Aerobic septic systems carry a maintenance contract with periodic inspections and ongoing cost; conventional systems need pumping on a cycle.
- Propane is a commodity you buy in bulk and it is priced by the market, not by a regulated tariff.
- Water hauling is a real thing during severe drought for properties with marginal wells.

Kendall County permits on-site sewage facilities and approves or denies an application within 30 days of receipt. Groundwater is managed by the **Cow Creek Groundwater Conservation District**, created by county voters in 2002; the county sits within the Hill Country Priority Groundwater Management Area over the Trinity Aquifer.

I will not publish typical dollar figures for well drilling, septic installation or propane here, because I haven't verified current local pricing and a stale number is worse than no number. Get current quotes from a local driller, installer and propane supplier — I'm happy to point you to people.

## Homeowners insurance

This is the line item that has moved most in the last several years and the one buyers underbudget most badly.

Texas runs well above the national average on homeowners insurance. Published 2026 statewide estimates vary by methodology and coverage assumption, generally landing somewhere in the **$3,300 to $4,900 per year** range for a typical policy, with some analyses putting the statewide average above $4,000 (NerdWallet and other 2026 industry analyses). Those are statewide figures, not Boerne figures, and I am not going to present a Boerne-specific average I can't source.

What actually drives your number here:

- **Hail.** This is the dominant peril in Central Texas and the main reason Texas premiums are high. Roof age and roof material drive your premium and sometimes your insurability. A 15-year-old composition roof can push you to an actual-cash-value roof settlement instead of replacement cost, which is a five-figure difference after a hailstorm.
- **Wildfire exposure.** On heavily wooded Hill Country acreage, brush proximity and defensible space matter to underwriters and to premium.
- **Distance to a fire station and ISO protection class.** Rural properties rate worse. This is a real and often large difference between an in-town house and one fifteen minutes out.
- **Water source.** Some carriers treat well-and-septic properties differently.
- **Claims history on the address**, not just on you. Pull the CLUE report.

**Do this during your option period, not after:** get a bound quote on the specific address from an agent who writes in Kendall County. Not an estimate, not a range — a quote on that address with that roof. I've seen quotes on comparable-looking Boerne properties come back thousands of dollars apart, and insurance is one of the few closing surprises that can actually kill a deal on debt-to-income.

## HOA and deed restrictions

Kendall County does not have zoning in unincorporated areas, which makes HOA documents disproportionately important — they are the only land-use control on a country property.

HOA dues vary enormously across this market, from modest annual assessments in older subdivisions to substantial monthly dues in gated, amenity-heavy developments with private roads, gates, pools or golf. I'm not going to publish an "average" — the range is so wide that an average would mislead you.

What to ask for, every time, during the option period:

1. **Current dues and payment frequency**, plus the assessment history for the last five years.
2. **Whether there is a special assessment pending or contemplated.** Private roads are the usual source. A subdivision that maintains its own roads has a capital liability that shows up eventually.
3. **The resale certificate and transfer fees**, which are paid at closing and are frequently a few hundred dollars that nobody mentioned.
4. **The full declaration of covenants, conditions and restrictions** — and read it for what it permits, not just what it forbids. Can your neighbor run a business? Keep livestock? Park an RV? Build a metal shop? In the absence of county zoning, the CCRs are the answer.
5. **Whether short-term rentals are permitted.** Many Kendall County subdivisions prohibit them by covenant regardless of what the city or county allows.
6. **Architectural control committee requirements**, if you plan to build, add on, or put up a barn or shop.
7. **The reserve balance**, if the association maintains infrastructure.

## Everything else

Kendall County's median household income was reported at $105,400 and the City of Boerne's at $91,947, with median ages of 43 and 40 respectively (Boerne Kendall County Economic Development Corporation). That's a comparatively affluent base, which shows up in local retail and restaurant pricing.

Boerne has a genuine grocery, healthcare, restaurant and services base for a town of about 24,000. What it doesn't have is San Antonio's depth — specialty medical, big-box variety, major-airport access and the long tail of retail are a drive down I-10. Budget the time, not just the gas.
