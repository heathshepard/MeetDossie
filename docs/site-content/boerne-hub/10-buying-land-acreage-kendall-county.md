# Buying Land and Acreage in Kendall County, TX

Kendall County is still, substantially, ranch country. The appraisal district's 2025 certified roll shows **$1.98 billion in agricultural market value inside Boerne ISD alone**, appraised at $9.2 million under productivity valuation — a productivity loss of $1.97 billion (Kendall Appraisal District, 2025 Certified Totals, as of Supplement 47, December 3, 2025). Roughly two-thirds of the taxable property accounts in Boerne ISD sit outside Boerne city limits.

Buying land here is a different transaction from buying a house. The failure modes are different, the diligence is different, and the cost of getting it wrong is larger — because unlike a house, land can turn out to be unbuildable.

## The first thing to understand: nobody is protecting you

The Kendall County Engineer's office puts it plainly:

> "Kendall County does not regulate land use and has not adopted zoning regulations in the unincorporated areas of the County."
>
> "While Kendall County has not adopted building codes for single family residential developments, the County has adopted a Fire Code administered by the Kendall County Fire Marshal..."
>
> *(Kendall County Engineer, Permits Required, Building Codes, Certificates of Occupancy, and Zoning Regulations Verification Letter, March 4, 2026)*

What the county **does** regulate: 911 addressing, subdivision of property, floodplain management, and permits for "placement or modification of driveways, any work within County-maintained right of way, outdoor lighting, on-site sewage facilities, and any site improvements whether within or outside of the floodplain." Development is defined broadly — "any man-made change to improved and unimproved real estate," including buildings, mining, dredging, filling, grading, paving, excavation, drilling, or storage of equipment or materials.

The Kendall County Health Department may also have requirements for certain multi-family and non-residential development. The Fire Marshal issues Certificates of Compliance and Certificates of Occupancy for certain multi-family and non-residential development.

The letter closes with the sentence that should govern your entire purchase: "The property owner must also abide by any subdivision conditions, covenants, restrictions and deed restrictions and home owners' association requirements in effect."

**Translation:** your deed restrictions are your zoning. If they don't prohibit it, your neighbor can do it. Read them completely, and read them for what they permit as much as what they forbid.

## Septic feasibility: the question that decides the deal

On vacant land, this is the first thing to answer and the most common reason a land purchase falls apart.

Kendall County permits on-site sewage facilities and approves or denies an application within 30 days of receipt. Statewide rules live in TCEQ's 30 TAC Chapter 285, with county environmental health departments acting as Authorized Agents handling day-to-day permitting — and local programs can be more stringent than the state minimum.

What you need:
- A **licensed site evaluator** to conduct a soil evaluation. Hill Country soil is frequently thin over limestone, and thin soil over rock drives you from a conventional system to an aerobic or other alternative system — a significant cost difference.
- Confirmation of setbacks from wells, property lines, water features and easements.
- A determination of what **system type** can be permitted, because that determines cost.
- On steep terrain, confirmation that a drain field or spray area actually fits where the house needs to go.

**Make the contract contingent on septic feasibility.** A land contract with no septic contingency on an unevaluated tract is a gamble, not a purchase.

For an existing home, get the OSSF permit on file, the system design, the maintenance and pumping history, the maintenance contract status on an aerobic system, and a dedicated septic inspection. The general home inspector's look at a septic system is not a septic inspection.

## Water: the other question that decides the deal

Kendall County sits inside the **Hill Country Priority Groundwater Management Area** — the region was declared a Critical Groundwater Area in 1990 — over the **Trinity Aquifer**. The **Cow Creek Groundwater Conservation District** was created by county voters on November 5, 2002, to conserve, preserve, recharge, protect and prevent waste of groundwater in the county.

Texas law exempts most single-household domestic wells and many agricultural and livestock uses from permitting. Exempt is not the same as unregulated: districts can still require registration and spacing, and can set limits for permitted users during drought.

Your diligence list on an existing well:
- **Yield test**, current, in writing. Not "the seller says it makes 15 gallons a minute."
- **Water quality test** — bacteriological and chemical.
- **Registration status** with Cow Creek GCD.
- **Well depth, casing depth and which formation it produces from.** A shallow well in the upper Trinity behaves very differently in drought than a deeper one.
- **Pump age and service history.**
- **Drought performance.** Ask neighbors. This is the one question where local memory beats any document.
- If it's a **shared well**, read the shared-well agreement line by line: who pays for the pump, who controls access, what happens when the other party won't pay, and what the remedy is.

On raw land with no well, get a drilling quote and talk to a local driller about what depth and yield is typical *on that specific side of the county*. It is not uniform.

Also confirm whether the tract is within a **water supply corporation** or **Kendall County WCID #1** service area — WCID #1 carries its own tax rate of 0.166106 (Kendall Appraisal District, 2025), and service availability and connection fees are their own investigation.

## Agricultural valuation: worth real money, and easy to lose

A 1-d-1 open-space agricultural valuation is the difference between paying tax on market value and paying tax on productivity value. In Boerne ISD, ag land carried $1.98 billion in market value and $9.2 million in productivity value in 2025. That is the scale of what's at stake.

From the **Kendall County Ag Use Manual**, the district's own published guidelines:

**History requirement:** the land must have been used principally for agriculture or timber production for **five of the preceding seven years**.

**Degree of intensity, by enterprise:**

| Enterprise | Kendall County minimum |
|---|---|
| Cow/calf | Minimum 3 calves and 10 acres |
| Sheep | Ewes plus 1 ram on 10 acres |
| Goats | 6 does and 1 buck, minimum 10 acres |
| Exotics | Minimum 10 acres, wire mesh fencing |
| Beekeeping | Not less than 5 nor more than 20 acres; minimum 6 hives |
| Orchard / vineyard | Minimum 3 acres |
| Market garden | Minimum 3 acres — family gardens do not qualify |
| Wildlife management | Minimum of three approved practices |

**Stocking rates by range type:**

| Range type | Carrying capacity |
|---|---|
| Improved Grass Range | 1 animal unit per 5 acres |
| Native Range I | 1 AU per 10 acres |
| Native Range II | 1 AU per 25 acres |
| Native Range III (oak) | 1 AU per 35 acres |
| Native Range III (cedar) | 1 AU per 50 acres |

An animal unit is a 1,000-pound cow.

Those cedar and oak numbers are the practitioner detail buyers miss. A 40-acre cedar-covered tract at 1 AU per 50 acres does not carry enough capacity to meet a cow/calf degree-of-intensity standard on its own. **Acreage alone does not qualify you.** Range condition does.

### The rollback

If you buy land under an ag valuation and stop the qualifying use, you can trigger a **rollback of back taxes**. On a tract carrying a productivity valuation that's a fraction of market value, that liability can be substantial.

Decide before you close:
- Are you continuing the existing use? Then get the lease, the livestock arrangement or the wildlife plan documented and in place at closing — not "sometime after we move in."
- Are you converting to residential? Then price the rollback into your offer and know the number before you sign.
- **Wildlife management** is the common path for buyers who want the valuation without running livestock. It requires the land to already qualify under 1-d-1 and a Texas Parks & Wildlife-compliant plan implementing a minimum of three approved practices. Get a wildlife biologist involved before closing, not after the first tax notice.

## Access, easements and the survey

Order a **new survey**. An old one that predates a fence line, a pond, an outbuilding or a neighbor's improvement will cost you far more in a boundary dispute than the survey saved.

What the survey and the title commitment's Schedule B need to establish:
- **Legal access**, not just a gravel road that has always been there. A tract with only historical access and no recorded easement is a real problem.
- **Easement locations** — pipeline, electric, water, drainage. A pipeline easement through the middle of your building site is the most expensive thing you can discover after closing.
- **Encroachments** in either direction.
- **Floodplain** boundaries relative to the building site.
- **Mineral and surface rights.** In Texas these sever, and in a county with any oil, gas or aggregate interest the surface owner can end up with an operator's surface rights they didn't expect. Ask what conveys.

## Floodplain and drainage

Kendall County administers floodplain management and requires permits for site improvements whether inside or outside the floodplain. Cibolo Creek and its tributaries have flooded the Boerne area repeatedly, and flood zone designation varies parcel by parcel — sometimes within the same subdivision.

Pull the FEMA FIRM panel for the exact parcel. Then go further, because FEMA maps do not capture every risk in Central Texas:
- Walk the property and find the drainage. Where does water go in a hard rain?
- **Where is the low-water crossing between the building site and the paved road?** A property that's dry but unreachable eight times a year is a real problem that no flood map shows you.
- Ask about the 2013 and earlier events specifically. People here remember.

## Dark sky lighting

Kendall County requires a **permit prior to the installation of any outdoor lights**, and regulated outdoor lighting must use International Dark-Sky Association compliant products installed per manufacturer or IDA specifications. The county adopted a night sky resolution in January 2020 and has pursued International Dark Sky Community designation. The City of Boerne has its own dark-sky regulations in its Unified Development Code.

If you're planning an arena, a lit sport court, security lighting on a shop or extensive landscape lighting, check the requirements before you design and budget. This is one of the genuinely good things about living here — the night sky in Kendall County is worth protecting — but it is a permit, not a suggestion.

## Utilities and infrastructure cost

Outside city limits there are no city utilities. Electric service comes from a cooperative — Bandera Electric Cooperative and Pedernales Electric Cooperative both serve portions of Kendall County, and the City of Boerne's own PUC-defined electric service area extends somewhat beyond the city limits, so verify who serves the specific parcel.

Budget lines people forget on raw land:
- Electric **service extension** to a remote building site. This can be a five-figure number and it is quoted by the cooperative, not estimated by your builder.
- **Driveway and culvert** — permitted by the county.
- **Propane tank** and initial fill.
- **Well** drilling, casing, pump, pressure tank, and a line to the house.
- **Septic** system, sized to the house.
- **Fencing**, which on acreage is expensive and is frequently in worse shape than it looks from the road.
- **Brush management**, particularly cedar, which is ongoing rather than one-time.

## Taxes on land

Combined 2025 rates for unincorporated Kendall County (Kendall Appraisal District):

| Stack | Rate |
|---|---|
| Kendall County + Boerne ISD + Cow Creek | 1.3929 |
| Kendall County + Comfort ISD + Cow Creek | 1.3148 |
| Add Kendall County MUD #1 | 2.0429 |
| Add Miralomas MUD | 2.3929 |
| Comfort ISD + Kendall County WCID #1 + Cow Creek | 1.480906 |

Note that **neither the City of Boerne nor Kendall County offers a general residence homestead exemption** — your only general homestead exemption is the $140,000 school district amount. On land you're holding without a residence, you have no homestead exemption at all, which is exactly why the ag valuation matters so much here.
