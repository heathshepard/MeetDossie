# Boerne, TX Real Estate Guide

I work Boerne listings on both sides of the deal — as of this writing I've got an active seller listing in the 78015 zip. Here's what the market actually looks like right now, pulled straight from MLS, not a syndicated estimate.

## What Boerne actually is for a buyer

Boerne is a Hill Country town on I-10, about 30 miles northwest of downtown San Antonio — not a subdivision-sprawl suburb. It has its own economy (tourism, Main Street retail), its own school district, and a price level well above the San Antonio metro median. Most buyers here are trading commute time for lot size, schools, or quiet. Some are retirees or remote workers buying for the town itself, no commute involved.

The zips that cover Boerne — 78006 and 78015 — span two different products: incorporated city lots with sewer and city services, and unincorporated Kendall County acreage on well and septic. Which one you're looking at changes your tax rate, your STR rights, and your closing costs. More on that below.

## Neighborhoods and areas — honest tradeoffs

**Historic Downtown / Old Boerne.** Walkable Main Street, established trees, smaller lots, older stock mixed with renovations. City water, sewer, full in-city tax rate. You're paying for location, not square footage.

**West Boerne master-planned communities (Esperanza, Johnson Ranch, and similar).** Newer construction, HOA fees, amenity centers, consistent finish quality, smaller lots than the acreage product further out. Most new-construction inventory sits here, so ask about builder incentives before offering on resale in the same subdivision.

**Fair Oaks Ranch.** Its own incorporated city, not Boerne, but grouped with it in most searches. Larger lots, its own services and tax structure — don't assume Boerne's city rate applies here.

**Unincorporated Kendall County acreage (Cordillera Ranch and similar).** Larger lots, more privacy, no city zoning — but well, septic, and often propane instead of municipal utilities. Lower combined tax rate (no city line), but you maintain the well/septic yourself and some areas rely on volunteer fire coverage. HOA/POA deed restrictions here are often stricter than city zoning, especially on short-term rentals — read them before assuming county means unregulated.

No "best" area here, only which tradeoff you want: walkability and a smaller lot, or acreage and utilities you maintain yourself. I'll pull actual comps for whichever specific area you're looking at — the numbers above are Boerne-wide.

## Schools

Boerne ISD is the district covering both 78006 and 78015 in most (not all) of the incorporated city and surrounding county — Fair Oaks Ranch and some outlying county areas can zone differently, so confirm the specific address before writing an offer. Boerne ISD campuses:

- **Elementary:** Cibolo Creek, Kendall, Curington, Van Raub, Fabra, Viola Wilson, Fair Oaks Ranch, Herff
- **Middle:** Voss Middle School, Boerne Middle School North, Boerne Middle School South
- **High:** Boerne High School, Champion High School
- Boerne Academy (alternative campus)

Which high school a given address feeds is attendance-zone-specific and has shifted as the district has grown — I verify current zoning against the address, not the neighborhood name, before I tell a buyer which school their kids would attend.

## Commute to San Antonio

Boerne sits on I-10, roughly 30 miles northwest of downtown San Antonio — a real commute, not a short one. Figure anywhere from about 35 minutes off-peak to well over an hour at rush hour, depending on where in Boerne you start and where you're headed (downtown, the medical center, Randolph/Lackland, and the north side all drive differently). The I-10/1604 interchange is the predictable bottleneck. If your job has any flexibility on hours, that gap matters here more than almost anywhere else in the metro — test-drive your actual commute at your actual commute time before you commit, not just a map estimate.

**Combined rate, in-city Boerne (City + County + Boerne ISD + Cow Creek):** $1.8645 per $100. On a $600,000 home, that's roughly $11,187/year before any exemptions.

**Combined rate, unincorporated county + Boerne ISD (no city tax):** $1.3929 per $100. On the same $600,000 home, that's roughly $8,357/year before exemptions — about $2,800/year less than the in-city rate, purely from not paying the City of Boerne line.

**Disabled veteran exemption.** Texas gives veterans property tax relief on a sliding scale under Tax Code §11.22: $5,000-$12,000 off, depending on VA disability rating (10%-90%). A veteran rated **100% disabled** qualifies for a full, total exemption on their homestead under §11.131 — not a discount, zero property tax. This changes your real carrying cost more than any other line item here, and it's worth confirming with Kendall CAD before ruling a house out on tax cost alone.

## Short-term rentals: city limits vs. unincorporated county

These are two different rule sets, and mixing them up is a common and expensive mistake.

**Inside Boerne city limits (and its ETJ):** allowed but regulated. Per the City's Short-Term Rental ordinance (ci.boerne.tx.us/2444/Short-Term-Rentals): a permit ($200, valid 2 years, $200 renewal), a safety inspection ($100, city-limits only — ETJ exempt), and liability insurance ($500K/occurrence, $1M aggregate minimum). In residential zones, a new STR can't sit within 200 feet of another on the same block face. STRs are barred from commercial zones except the Historic, Downtown, and River Road overlays. Occupancy caps at two people per bedroom plus two; no event hosting.

**Unincorporated Kendall County (outside city limits and the ETJ):** no county-level STR permit or ordinance turned up on the county's own site — consistent with how little zoning authority Texas counties generally have over unincorporated land. That doesn't mean no rules apply: HOA/POA deed restrictions (common and often strict in the gated acreage communities), well/septic capacity, and county fire code all still bind you. I could not verify whether Kendall County levies its own Hotel Occupancy Tax on unincorporated STRs — many Texas counties do. Confirm with the Kendall County Tax Assessor-Collector before you close if STR income is part of the plan.

If short-term rental income is part of how you're underwriting a Boerne purchase, tell me before we write the offer — it changes which zip and which specific property make sense.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Boerne, Texas a good place to buy a house right now?**
Boerne has roughly 4.4 months of housing inventory as of September 2026, which leans toward a seller's market but isn't extreme. Median sale price over the trailing 12 months is running about 3% above the prior 12 months — steady growth, not a spike. Homes are selling at about 97% of list price on average.

**How far is Boerne from San Antonio?**
Boerne is on I-10, about 30 miles northwest of downtown San Antonio. Commute time varies widely by time of day and destination — roughly 35 minutes off-peak, over an hour during rush hour to downtown or the north side, longer to bases like Lackland on the south side.

**What is the property tax rate in Boerne, Texas?**
For 2025, the combined rate for a home inside Boerne city limits (City of Boerne + Kendall County + Boerne ISD + Cow Creek Groundwater District) is $1.8645 per $100 of assessed value. Outside city limits in unincorporated Kendall County, the combined rate (no city tax) is $1.3929 per $100. Source: Kendall County Appraisal District, 2025 adopted tax rates.

**Do 100% disabled veterans pay property tax in Boerne or Kendall County?**
No. Under Texas Tax Code §11.131, a veteran with a 100% VA disability rating qualifies for a full, total exemption from property taxes on their homestead — not a partial discount. Veterans with a lower disability rating (10%-90%) qualify for a smaller exemption on a sliding scale ($5,000-$12,000) under §11.22 instead.

**Can I do a short-term rental (Airbnb/VRBO) in Boerne?**
Inside Boerne city limits, yes, with a permit: $200 registration good for 2 years, a $100 safety inspection (city-limits properties only), $500K/$1M minimum liability insurance, and zoning limits (no new STR within 200 feet of another on the same block face in residential zones, and STRs are barred from most commercial zones outside the historic/downtown/River Road overlays). Outside city limits in unincorporated Kendall County, there's no county STR permit that we found, but HOA deed restrictions and county Hotel Occupancy Tax rules (unverified as of this writing) may still apply — check both before you buy for that purpose.

**What school district is Boerne in?**
Most of Boerne, in both the 78006 and 78015 zip codes, is zoned to Boerne ISD, which has eight elementary campuses, three middle schools (Voss, Boerne Middle School North, and Boerne Middle School South), and two high schools (Boerne High School and Champion High School). Some outlying areas, including parts of Fair Oaks Ranch, can zone differently — always confirm the specific address, not just the neighborhood name.

**What's the difference between living in Boerne city limits and unincorporated Kendall County?**
In-city properties have municipal water and sewer, a higher combined tax rate (city tax layered on top of county and school), and are subject to the city's short-term rental ordinance if you plan to rent short-term. Unincorporated county properties typically run on well and septic, have a lower combined tax rate (no city line), and aren't subject to city zoning — but are often governed instead by strict HOA/POA deed restrictions in the larger gated communities.

**Why is the average home price so much higher than the median in Boerne?**
Over the trailing 12 months, the average sale price (~$740,675) runs about $138,000 above the median (~$602,600). That gap is driven by Boerne's higher end — acreage properties, new construction, and luxury gated communities like Cordillera Ranch — pulling the average upward. For a typical in-town or subdivision purchase, the median is the more representative number to budget against.
