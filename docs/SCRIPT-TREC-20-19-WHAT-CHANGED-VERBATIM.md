## READ THIS FIRST

**1. Turn the phone's preview off, or flip the phone so you can't see yourself.**
Watching your own face while you talk is what causes the blank. Cover the screen with a sticky
note if the app won't let you kill the preview. You'll see the footage in ten minutes anyway.

**2. Hit record and talk about nothing for three minutes before Card 1.**
The weather, what you had for lunch, the drive. Then **do not stop the recording** when the real
script starts. The first three minutes get deleted. Their only job is to get past the part where
the red dot is new.

**3. Flubs are free.**
Say the line wrong, stop, breathe, say the line again, keep rolling. Do not restart the take. Do
not apologize to the camera. Do not say "sorry, let me try that again" — that's another thirty
seconds of footage to cut around. Just say the line again, clean.

**4. One card per take is ideal. If you're in a groove, keep going.**
Don't break a run that's working to obey the structure.

## RECORDING SETUP

| | |
|---|---|
| **Camera position** | **6–8 feet back**, zoomed in to a medium shot. Distance is what hides the eye offset — at 3 feet a glance to the script is obvious, at 7 feet it reads as a think-beat. |
| **Script position** | Printed or on a screen **directly under the lens**. Not off to the side. Big type. |
| **Delivery** | **Land the last word of every sentence.** Trailing off is the number one tell that someone is reading ahead. Finish the word, then breathe. |
| **Chunks** | Three lines, then a blank line. Read a chunk, look up, say it. The blank line is where your eyes come back to. |
| **Room** | Soft furnishings — couch, rug, curtains. **NOT the laundry room.** Hard surfaces made the 09-21 recording hollow and no plugin removes reverb. |
| **Audio** | **Confirm the DJI lav is the phone's actual input source**, gain at 0. On 09-21 the mic was clipped on, visible in frame, and the file still had phone-mic audio. Check the camera app's input before take one. |
| **Wardrobe** | No over-ear headphones. No KW branding. |

# CARD 1 — THE NEW WATER DISCLOSURE

`[SCREEN: page 5, paragraph 7.I]`

There's a brand new paragraph in this contract.
Paragraph **seven-I**.
It did not exist in twenty-eighteen at all.

Seller's Disclosure About Groundwater
and Surface Water Rights.
It lives in Property Condition.

Not in the title notices.
That's where everybody keeps looking for it.
It's not there.

[pause]

Three boxes. You check **one**.
Buyer got it.
Buyer didn't get it, and the seller delivers in blank days.

Or, the seller isn't required to deliver it at all.
Box two is the one that hurts.
Read what it actually says.

If the buyer never receives it,
the buyer can terminate
**any time prior to the closing**.

Earnest money refunded.

[pause]

Any time before closing.
That is not your option period.
That's week four. Week five. The day before you fund.

And if the seller delivers it late?
Buyer gets **seven days** from receipt.
For any reason. Money back.

[pause]

Now box three. Seller not required.
That's not a freebie.
**All five** conditions have to be true.

And one of them is no water well.
In use — **or not in use**.
A capped well nobody's touched in years still counts.

You also have to name the water provider.
City, MUD, water supply corporation.
Naming it is part of the election.

[pause]

In a platted subdivision in town, on SAWS,
box three takes ten seconds.
Out on acreage with a well? That's a conversation.

# CARD 3 — WHERE THE COMPENSATION LINE WENT

`[SCREEN: split — 20-18 ¶12.A(1)(b) struck, 20-19 ¶12.B]`

Where did the seller contribution
to the buyer's agent go?

It moved.

[pause]

It is not in paragraph twelve-A anymore.
That muscle memory
is going to cost somebody a deal.

There's a whole new paragraph **twelve-B**.
It's called Brokerage Compensation.
And it runs both directions.

Twelve-B-one.
Seller pays toward what the **buyer**
owes the buyer's broker.

Twelve-B-two.
Buyer pays toward what the **seller**
owes the seller's broker.

Dollars, or a percent. Check one.

[pause]

Now read the bold sentence above those boxes.
These contributions apply to — and do **not** change —
what each side already owes their own broker.

So a seller contribution
is not a commission agreement.
Those are two different documents.

Your buyer rep says two and a half.
Seller contributes two.
Your client owes the rest.

Say that at the table.
Not at closing.

[pause]

And twelve-A-one-B, the general concession line,
now specifically **excludes** brokerage compensation.
Two separate buckets. Don't stack them into one number.

Last thing here.
The old paragraph twelve-B
let a party terminate if an expense ran over.

That one is **deleted**.
It didn't move.
It's gone.

Twelve-C is a narrower thing
about FHA and VA non-allowables.
Different animal entirely.

# CARD 4 — PARAGRAPH EIGHT

`[SCREEN: ¶8]`

Paragraph eight.

It used to be Brokers and Sales Agents,
with an A and a B.
Now it's **Broker or Sales Agent Disclosure**.

One block. No letters.
The old eight-B is gone.
That was the brokers' fees line.

[pause]

What's left is the licensee-as-a-party disclosure.
And it's broader
than most agents think it is.

You. Your spouse. Your parent. Your child.
A business entity you own
more than **ten percent** of.

A trust you're the trustee of,
or that you or your family benefit from.
All of that triggers it.

[pause]

In writing.
**Before** you enter into a contract of sale.

Before.
Not at closing.
Not in the MLS remarks.
