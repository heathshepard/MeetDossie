E-Signatures Are Coming to Dossie

Not shipped yet. E-signatures are on the roadmap for a Q2 2026 beta — here's what she'll do when they land.

By Heath Shepard, Texas REALTOR® Updated 2026-08-07
meetdossie.com/workspace

A design preview of the Send for Signature flow. Not yet available in your account.

E-signatures are not live yet. They're listed on Dossie's roadmap as a Q2 2026 beta, and nothing on this page is available in your account today. It's here so you know what's being built and can tell us whether it's the right shape. Until it ships, Dossie files the signed copies you send through your existing e-sign tool.

1. Pick the document

The plan: any document already in the dossier — amendment, addendum, disclosure — gets sent for signature from the same place it lives, instead of exporting it first.

2. Role-based prefill

Dossie already knows who's the buyer's agent and who's the seller's agent on a file, so the intent is to prefill the right recipient with the right fields rather than dragging signature boxes onto a PDF by hand.

3. Send, track, file

The goal is for the executed copy to land back in the dossier on its own once every party has signed — no download-and-reupload step.

Timing: Q2 2026 beta, priced at $0.50 per envelope as an add-on. Dates on a roadmap are intentions, not commitments — we'll say so plainly here if they move.

Dossie is worth having before e-sign lands.

Frequently asked

Can I send a document for signature in Dossie today? +
No. E-signatures are not released yet — they're on the roadmap for a Q2 2026 beta. Keep using your current e-sign tool and upload the executed copy to the dossier; Dossie files and reads it like any other document.
When will e-signatures be available? +
The current target is a Q2 2026 beta, offered as a $0.50-per-envelope add-on. That's a plan, not a promise — if the date moves, this page moves with it.
Will I need a separate e-signature account? +
The intent is no — Send for Signature is being built to run inside Dossie, so there's no second tool to log into. That's the design goal, not a shipped feature.
