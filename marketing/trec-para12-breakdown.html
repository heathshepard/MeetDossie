Paragraph 12, Box by Box

What Settlement Expenses and Brokerage Compensation actually say in the One to Four Family Residential Contract (20-19) — every box, what's printed, what's a blank, and the one sentence agents get backwards.

By Heath Shepard, Texas REALTOR® · Updated 2026-10-01 · Source: TREC Form 20-19 (05-04-2026), Paragraph 12
Scope: this page covers Paragraph 12 of TREC 20-19 only — Settlement and Other Expenses, including Brokerage Compensation. It describes what the form's printed language says, nothing more. It is not legal advice, it does not cover your buyer representation agreement or listing agreement (those are separate documents this paragraph refers to but doesn't contain), and it does not address the 2024 NAR settlement or any broker policy beyond the four corners of this form. ⚠ The sentence everyone gets backwards
The contract does not set what you're paid
¶12.B
Governs both contribution boxes below
Printed, verbatim, opening ¶12.B: "Brokerage compensation is not set by law and is fully negotiable. Each party shall pay their respective brokers pursuant to the terms of the parties' obligations to compensate their brokers contained in separate written agreements. The contributions under 12B(1) and 12B(2) below shall be applied to and shall not change the parties' obligations to pay compensation pursuant to those agreements."
What this means: your buyer representation agreement or listing agreement — a separate signed document, not this contract — is what actually obligates each party to pay their broker. ¶12.B(1) and (2) below are contributions one party can make toward the other side's broker. The form says in writing that a contribution does not change what's owed under the separate agreement. Example: if a seller contributes 2% under ¶12.B(1) but the buyer rep agreement says 3%, the buyer still owes the remaining 1% at closing — the contribution didn't satisfy the agreement, it just paid part of it. That conversation needs to happen before the closing table, not at it.
Verified against ¶12.B of the specimen 20-19 PDF, quoted directly above — not summarized.
1. Settlement Expenses — ¶12.A
Who pays what at or before closing. Not brokerage compensation — that's ¶12.B, below.
Box: Seller's Expenses
¶12.A(1)(a)
Seller owes it
Printed, fixed list — not a blank: releases of existing liens (including prepayment penalties and recording fees), release of Seller's loan liability, tax statements or certificates, preparation of the deed, one-half of the escrow fee, and other expenses payable by Seller under this contract.
All of this "must be paid at or prior to closing" per the ¶12.A lead-in sentence.
Box: Seller's concession toward Buyer's Expenses
¶12.A(1)(b)
Seller may agree to this — a negotiated cap, not automatic
An amount not to exceed $_____ (blank) to be applied to Buyer's Expenses — printed language explicitly adds: "other than brokerage compensation or contributions under Paragraph 12B below."
This box cannot be used to fund the ¶12.B brokerage contributions — the form draws that line itself. It only offsets Buyer's Expenses under ¶12.A(2), below.
Box: Buyer's Expenses
¶12.A(2)
Buyer owes it
Printed, fixed list — not a blank: appraisal fees; loan application fees; origination charges; credit reports; preparation of loan documents; interest on the notes from date of disbursement to one month prior to the first monthly payment; recording fees; copies of easements and restrictions; loan title policy with lender-required endorsements; loan-related inspection fees; photos; amortization schedules; one-half of escrow fee; all prepaid items (flood and hazard insurance premiums, reserve deposits, ad valorem taxes, special governmental assessments); final compliance inspection; courier fee; repair inspection; underwriting fee; wire transfer fee; expenses incident to any loan; PMI, VA Funding Fee, or FHA MIP as required by the lender; and other expenses payable by Buyer under this contract.
Same "must be paid at or prior to closing" rule as Seller's Expenses above.
2. Brokerage Compensation — ¶12.B
Two contribution boxes. Read the trap box above first — these do not set what either broker is owed.
Box 1: Seller contributes toward Buyer's broker
¶12.B(1)
Seller's option — check one box only
Printed instruction: "check one box only" between $_____ (a flat dollar amount) or _____% of the Sales Price (blank), "to be applied toward the brokerage compensation owed by Buyer to Buyer's broker."
This is the box most "seller is paying the buyer's agent" conversations actually point to. It's a contribution toward whatever the buyer already owes under their buyer rep agreement — see the trap box above.
Box 2: Buyer contributes toward Seller's broker
¶12.B(2)
Buyer's option — check one box only
Same structure, mirrored: "check one box only" between $_____ (blank) or _____% of the Sales Price (blank), "to be applied toward the brokerage compensation owed by Seller to Seller's broker."
Printed symmetrically to ¶12.B(1) in the form — a buyer-side contribution toward what the seller already owes their own broker under the listing agreement. Same non-substitution rule applies.
When these boxes must be paid
¶12.B
Both boxes
Printed, closing the ¶12.B lead-in: "The following contributions must be paid at closing." Note this is narrower than ¶12.A's "at or prior to closing" — the contribution boxes specifically say at closing.
3. Expense Limitation — ¶12.C
Only triggers when a government loan program prohibits a buyer-paid charge
Box: Order of operations when a loan program blocks a Buyer charge
¶12.C
Governs the ¶12.A(1)(b) seller concession only
Printed: "If a governmental loan program regulation prohibits Buyer from paying charges or fees, then the amount of any concession in 12A(1)(b) shall be first applied to pay such prohibited expenses and then to other Buyer's Expenses" — and explicitly: "but not brokerage compensation or contribution."
Order matters: prohibited expenses first, then other Buyer's Expenses. Either way, this concession can never flow into ¶12.B compensation or contributions — the form repeats that exclusion a second time here.
Flagged for Hadley — form is silent, not independently resolved What happens if a contribution exceeds what's owed under the separate agreement? ¶12.B states contributions "shall not change the parties' obligations to pay compensation" under the buyer rep/listing agreement — that covers the shortfall case (contribution less than owed) explicitly. It does not say what happens to the excess if a contribution is larger than the obligation — refund to the contributing party, credit elsewhere, or something the buyer rep/listing agreement itself has to address. I did not find language in ¶12 answering this, and I'm not guessing at an answer from outside the four corners of the form. Recommend Hadley confirm whether this is answered in the standard buyer rep agreement (TREC or TAR) rather than the sales contract, or whether it's genuinely a gap practitioners handle case by case.
