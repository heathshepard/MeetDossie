# Citation trail — batch 16: FIRPTA Withholding (Texas guide)

Guide: `marketing/guides-data/texas-firpta-withholding-guide.json`
Topic: FIRPTA (Foreign Investment in Real Property Tax Act) withholding — IRC §1445 — on a sale by a foreign person.

## Primary sources verified directly (2026-08-09)

1. **26 U.S.C. §1445 (statute text)** — law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1445
   - §1445(a): general rule — transferee must withhold **15 percent** of the amount realized on disposition of a U.S. real property interest by a foreign person.
   - §1445(b)(5): **full exemption** — no withholding required if the property is acquired by the transferee "for use by him as a residence" AND the amount realized does not exceed **$300,000**.
   - §1445(c)(4): **reduced 10 percent rate** — applies when the property is acquired by the transferee for use as a residence, the amount realized exceeds $300,000 but does not exceed **$1,000,000**, and the (b)(5) exemption doesn't apply.
   - Confirms it is the **transferee's (buyer's) intended use** that controls residence classification for both the exemption and the reduced rate — not the seller's status or intent.
   - §1445(e): separate, higher withholding rates apply to certain foreign-corporation/partnership/trust/estate distributions (tied to the top corporate/individual rate under §1 / §11(b)) — out of scope for a simple individual-seller sale, flagged in guide as "ask a CPA" territory rather than detailed.

2. **IRS — "FIRPTA Withholding"** — irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/firpta-withholding
   - Confirms the 15% general rate (raised from 10% for dispositions after 2/16/2016 — noted so the guide doesn't cite a stale 10% "old" rate as current).
   - Confirms the $300,000 full exemption and reduced-rate band up to $1,000,000, both conditioned on **buyer's residence-use intent**, consistent with the statute.
   - Confirms 21% rate context for certain foreign-corporation distributions (not used in guide body — too far outside a residential resale scope, mentioned only as "other rates exist for entity distributions").
   - Lists governing forms: Form 8288, Form 8288-A, Form 8288-B.

3. **IRS — "Definitions of Terms and Procedures Unique to FIRPTA"** — irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/definitions-of-terms-and-procedures-unique-to-firpta
   - Confirms: "The buyer (transferee) ... is the withholding agent" — buyer bears the withholding obligation and the liability exposure if it's skipped, not the seller.
   - Confirms who counts as a "foreign person" for FIRPTA purposes: nonresident alien individuals; foreign corporations (unless they've made a domestic-election); foreign partnerships, trusts, or estates. Resident aliens are explicitly **not** foreign persons under FIRPTA.
   - Used to support the guide's brief explainer on how foreign-person status is determined (residency test, entity formation, not citizenship alone) — with explicit direction to a CPA/tax attorney for a client's actual determination, not a bright-line test the guide itself applies.

4. **IRS — "Reporting and Paying Tax on U.S. Real Property Interests"** — irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/reporting-and-paying-tax-on-u-s-real-property-interests
   - Confirms the **20-day** deadline: Form 8288 (and attached 8288-A per foreign transferor) must be filed and withheld tax remitted "by the 20th day after the date of the disposition."
   - Confirms Form 8288-B (application for withholding certificate) lets a foreign seller apply to reduce or eliminate withholding before closing; if pending at closing, withholding still must be collected/escrowed but remittance to the IRS is deferred until 20 days after the IRS rules on the certificate.
   - Confirms interest/penalties attach starting the 21st day after transfer if remittance is late (including cases where an 8288-B was filed principally to delay payment).

## Certification of non-foreign status — note on sourcing

The guide describes the seller's written certification that they are NOT a foreign person (the standard way a buyer avoids the withholding obligation entirely when the seller is, in fact, a U.S. person) as a **"Certification of Non-Foreign Status"** under Treas. Reg. §1.1445-2(b) — this is the commonly-used industry term (also appears on TREC/title-company closing paperwork) for the seller's sworn statement of U.S. taxpayer status, signed under penalty of perjury. It is **distinct from IRS Form W-9** (which is a general taxpayer-ID request form, not a FIRPTA-specific certification) — the guide corrects the common mix-up between the two rather than treating them as interchangeable, per Heath's brief. This distinction is standard title-industry practice; it was not re-verified against a specific numbered IRS form because the IRS does not publish a standalone numbered form for this certification — it's a regulatory requirement (26 CFR §1.1445-2(b)(2)) satisfied by an affidavit, not an IRS form number. Flagged in the guide as something a title company/closing attorney supplies the standard language for — not something the guide reproduces as boilerplate.

## What the guide deliberately does NOT claim

- Does not give a bright-line test for determining a specific seller's foreign-person status — points to CPA/tax attorney per Hadley's standing rule on tax matters.
- Does not address FIRPTA withholding on entity distributions (§1445(e)) in depth — flagged as a separate, more complex scenario needing tax counsel.
- Does not state a specific penalty percentage/rate for late remittance — IRS source referenced interest+penalties generally without a numeric rate on this page; guide says "interest and penalties" without inventing a number.
