# Citation trail — texas-real-estate-recovery-trust-account-guide

## Primary sources (pulled and read directly, 2026-08-09)

### Texas Occupations Code, Chapter 1101, Subchapter M — Real Estate Recovery Trust Account

Individual sections pulled and read via texas.public.law and codes.findlaw.com (both mirror
the official Texas Statutes text; codes.findlaw.com quotes were checked against
texas.public.law for consistency — no discrepancies found).

**§1101.601 — Real Estate Recovery Trust Account.** Full text pulled. Establishes the
account "to reimburse aggrieved persons who suffer actual damages caused by an act
described by Section 1101.602" committed by a license holder, certificate holder, or their
unlicensed employee/agent, while that person held the license/certificate.
Source: https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/occupations-code/occ-sect-1101-601.html

**§1101.602 — Entitlement to Reimbursement.** Full text pulled. Ties eligibility to conduct
described in §1101.652(a-1)(1)/(b) (license holders) or §1101.653(1)-(4) (certificate holders)
— i.e., conduct that is independently a statutory ground for license suspension/revocation,
not any breach-of-contract claim generally.
Source: https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/occupations-code/occ-sect-1101-602.html

**§1101.605 — Deadline for Action; Notice to Commission.** Full text pulled.
Subsection (a): *"An action for a judgment that may result in an order for payment from
the trust account may not be brought after the second anniversary of the date the cause
of action accrues."* **This is the confirmed 2-year statute of limitations to file the
underlying civil suit** — CONFIRMED against the task brief's claim.
Source: https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._occ._code_section_1101.605

**§1101.606 — Claim for Payment from Trust Account.** Full text pulled.
Subsection (b): *"If the aggrieved person does not schedule a hearing before the first
anniversary of the date the application was filed, recovery is waived."* **This is the
confirmed 1-year hearing-scheduling deadline / waiver provision** — CONFIRMED against
the task brief's claim. Also sets the sequence: final judgment → execution returned nulla
bona → judgment lien perfected → verified claim filed in the same court → 20-day wait
after written notice to TREC and the judgment debtor → apply to the court for payment.
Source: https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/occupations-code/occ-sect-1101-606.html

**§1101.607 — Issues at Hearing.** Full text pulled. At any hearing, the aggrieved person
must show the judgment rests on facts allowing recovery under the subchapter; that the
claimant isn't the judgment debtor's spouse, a license holder seeking their own
commission, or a first-degree relative of the debtor; that the debtor lacks sufficient
attachable assets; and the balance still owed after any asset sale.
Source: https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/occupations-code/occ-sect-1101-607.html

**§1101.610 — Payment Limits; Attorney's Fees.** Full text pulled.
(a): *"Payments from the trust account for claims, including attorney's fees, interest, and
court costs, arising out of a single transaction may not exceed a total of $125,000,
regardless of the number of claimants."*
(b): *"Payments from the trust account for claims based on judgments against a single
license or certificate holder may not exceed a total of $250,000 until the license or
certificate holder has reimbursed the trust account for all amounts paid."*
(e): a business entity and its designated broker count as a single license holder for this
cap. **CONFIRMED: $125,000 per-transaction / $250,000 per-license-holder caps, exactly
as stated in the task brief**, currently in effect (raised from $50,000/$100,000 by SB 1577,
effective 1/1/2024 — corroborated independently by the TREC-affiliated Texas Real Estate
Research Center article, see below).
Source: https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/occupations-code/title-7/subtitle-a/chapter-1101/subchapter-m/section-1101-610/
(fetched via texas.public.law mirror after law.justia.com blocked WebFetch with 403)

**Section index for Subchapter M** (1101.601–1101.615) cross-checked via
texas.public.law and law.justia.com listings — used to confirm no other section carries a
dollar cap or deadline that would supersede the above.

### Texas Occupations Code §1101.612 — Subrogation

Referenced (not independently re-pulled in full) via the TREC Complaint Overview Manual
and the TAMU Real Estate Research Center article, both of which quote it consistently:
once TREC pays a claim, TREC is subrogated to the judgment creditor's rights and has
priority on any later recovery from the license holder.

### Texas Occupations Code §1101.655 — License revocation after Trust Account payment

Referenced via the TAMU Real Estate Research Center article (see below): TREC must
revoke a license holder's license if the amount TREC paid from the Trust Account isn't
repaid in full within 31 days of TREC's notice; a new license can't issue until the amount
(plus accrued interest) is repaid.

## TREC's own materials

### TREC Complaint Overview Manual (Rev. September 2021)

PDF fetched directly from
https://www.trec.texas.gov/sites/default/files/pdf-forms/TREC%20Complaint%20Overview%20Manual.September.2021.pdf
and read as a document (WebFetch's HTML-extraction failed on this PDF — it was read
natively instead). This is the source of the guide's lead framing point. Exact quote, Section
II.B, p.4, footnoted to 22 Tex. Admin. Code §535.142(d):

> "Complaints regarding matters outside the jurisdiction of the Commission are closed
> without an investigation. For example, these types of complaints may include a
> commission dispute between a sales agent and their broker, a tenant complaint against a
> landlord who does not hold a real estate license, and any private dispute between the
> parties to a contract."

Also confirms (Section I.B, footnoted to §1101.204(c), Texas Occupations Code): the
**complaint** time limit (a wholly separate track from the Recovery Trust Account) is **4
years** from the date of the incident — included in the guide only as a contrast point, not
conflated with the Trust Account's 2-year suit deadline.

**CONFIRMED: this is the primary-source basis for the guide's corrective framing** — that
the ordinary TREC complaint process does not exist to get a consumer's money back, and
a "private dispute between the parties to a contract" complaint is closed on jurisdictional
grounds without investigation. The Recovery Trust Account is a structurally separate,
narrower remedy requiring an actual civil judgment first — confirmed independently by the
TAMU Real Estate Research Center article's explicit statement: "Filing an application for
payment from the Trust Account is completely separate and independent of filing a
complaint with TREC's Enforcement division. Although a consumer may file both, they are
two unrelated independent processes."

**CONFIRMED: the prior research pass's cited figures (830 payments / $16,945,970 since

not taken on faith. **However, this is TREC's own most recent published cumulative
figure I was able to locate as of this research pass (2026-08-09)** — I found no newer
TREC-published aggregate statistics page or updated payment-history table anywhere on
trec.texas.gov, and TREC's public hub page for the Trust Account does not carry a
statistics section at all. The guide dates the figure explicitly ("as of TREC's May 2023
staff report, the most recent the agency has published") rather than presenting it as
current-as-of-today, per the "don't just repeat stale figures" instruction — it is presented
honestly as the latest available number, not as a live 2026 count.

Also from this article (used for supporting context, not headline claims):
- Fund history: created mid-1970s with a $1M balance, replacing surety-bond
  requirements; funded by biennial-renewal assessments plus TREC administrative
  penalties.
- Real Estate Inspection Recovery Fund was **eliminated effective January 1, 2024** by
  HB 1363, because inspectors are statutorily required to carry liability insurance and the
  inspection fund had paid only 3 claims in the prior 10 years. (Used in the guide to clarify
  that today's Trust Account covers brokers/agents/ERW agents — not inspectors, who
  no longer have a separate recovery fund.)
- SB 1577 also gave TREC rulemaking authority to set/collect fees to help maintain the
  Trust Account, and repealed the account's old statutory $1M floor / $3.5M ceiling —
  reproduced narrowly in the guide (the $3.5M reversion-to-general-fund figure is still
  operative per the TAMU article's own later paragraph, so both statements were kept
  as the article states them rather than treated as contradictory).

### Cantey Hanger LLP practitioner article, May 2025 (secondary, cross-checked only)

https://canteyhanger.com/news/2025/05/trec-recovery-fund-offers-meaningful-recovery-against-license-holders/
— fetched directly. Confirms, independently of the TAMU article: $125,000 per-transaction

## What did NOT change from the task brief

Every figure in the task brief was independently verified against primary sources and
confirmed accurate:
- $125,000 per transaction / $250,000 per license holder, effective 1/1/2024 via SB 1577 —
  CONFIRMED (§1101.610(a)-(b), corroborated by TAMU article and Cantey Hanger).
- 2-year deadline to file suit from the act/omission — CONFIRMED (§1101.605(a)).
- 2-year deadline to file the TREC claim after judgment — CONFIRMED (TAMU article,
  cross-checked against TREC's own site language surfaced in search: "you must file
  your lawsuit in court within two years... the consumer must file a claim with TREC
  within two years of the date of the civil court judgment"). Note: the operative statute
  (§1101.606) does not itself spell out this second 2-year clock in the subsections read
  directly — it is stated consistently by both TREC's own public-facing language and the
  TAMU legal-authored article, so it is presented in the guide as TREC's stated
  requirement without over-claiming a specific subsection citation for that particular
  clock.
- 1-year hearing-scheduling waiver — CONFIRMED (§1101.606(b), quoted verbatim above).
- 830 payments / $16,945,970.10 / avg $20,417 since inception — CONFIRMED by direct
  primary-source read of TREC's own payment-history table, dated to TREC's May 2023
  staff report (the most recent TREC has published).
- "Private dispute between the parties to a contract" closed without investigation —
  CONFIRMED verbatim from TREC's own Complaint Overview Manual, Section II.B.

## Ambiguity flagged for the writer / reviewer

The exact statutory subsection establishing the second 2-year clock (claim filed within 2
years of judgment) was not pinned down to a single cite the way the 2-year suit deadline
(§1101.605(a)) and the 1-year hearing waiver (§1101.606(b)) were. It's stated identically by
TREC's own public materials and by the TAMU/State-Bar-authored article, so it's treated
as reliable, but a future pass with full access to §1101.606(a)'s cross-references or 22 TAC
Chapter 535's recovery-fund rules could tighten the citation further if it ever matters for a
hostile-audience (attorney) read.
