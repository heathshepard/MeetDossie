# Citation trail — military-pcs-orders-real-estate-contract-guide

## Primary sources (pulled and read directly, 2026-08-09)

### 50 U.S. Code § 3952 — Evictions and distress
Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/50/3952 (Cornell LII, mirrors the official U.S. Code)

Full text pulled via WebFetch. Key language, subsection (a)(1):

> "a contract by the servicemember for—(A) the purchase of real or personal property (including a motor vehicle); or (B) the lease or bailment of such property, may not be rescinded or terminated for a breach of terms of the contract occurring before or during that person's military service, nor may the property be repossessed for such breach without a court order."

Subsection (a)(2) — applies only where a deposit or installment was paid **before** the servicemember entered military service.

Subsection (b) — criminal penalty (fine and/or up to 1 year imprisonment) for a party who unlawfully repossesses/resumes possession in violation of (a).

Subsection (c) — court authority to condition contract termination on repayment of installments, stay proceedings, or issue other equitable orders.

**Finding: §3952 runs in the OPPOSITE direction from a buyer's-right-to-cancel theory.** It protects a servicemember purchaser/lessee AGAINST the other party (here, effectively a seller enforcing a breach) rescinding or repossessing without a court order. It is a shield against the servicemember being penalized for a contract breach connected to military service — not a sword the servicemember can use to walk away from a purchase contract voluntarily because of new orders. Confirmed by direct reading of the statutory text, not secondhand summary.

### 50 U.S. Code § 3955 — Termination of residential or motor vehicle leases
Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/50/3955 (Cornell LII)

Full text pulled via WebFetch. Confirmed scope, subsection (a):

> Applies to "a lease of premises occupied, or intended to be occupied, by a servicemember or a servicemember's dependents" (residential leases) and "a lease of a motor vehicle used, or intended to be used, by a servicemember or a servicemember's dependents for personal or business transportation" (vehicle leases).

Termination triggers for **residential leases**, subsection (b):
1. Lease executed by someone who thereafter enters military service during its term; or
2. Servicemember "receives military orders for a permanent change of station" or "to deploy with a military unit... for a period of not less than 90 days"; or
3. A stop-movement order preventing occupancy, issued in connection with a PCS or 90-day-plus deployment.

**Finding: §3955 is a lease-termination statute only.** Its text applies to "premises occupied... by a servicemember" under a lease — it does not use the words "purchase," "sale," "buyer," "vendee," or "contract for deed" anywhere in the operative termination language. There is no purchase-contract equivalent to §3955 anywhere in the SCRA. This is the single most important distinction for the guide: PCS/deployment orders of 90+ days DO create a statutory termination right for a *residential lease* — they create NO such right for a *purchase contract*.

## TREC contract check

### TREC No. 20-19, One to Four Family Residential Contract (Resale)
Source: https://www.trec.texas.gov/sites/default/files/pdf-forms/20-19.pdf (official TREC PDF)

Attempted direct text extraction via WebFetch; the PDF returned as embedded font/stream data rather than cleanly extractable text (common for TREC's form PDFs). Cross-checked via web search for any secondary source, legal commentary, CE material, or title-company explainer referencing a military/PCS/deployment/servicemember/SCRA clause anywhere in the TREC 20-19 (or its immediate predecessor 20-18/20-17) form or its standard addenda (Third Party Financing Addendum, Addendum for Sale of Other Property, HOA Addendum, Appraisal Addendum, etc.). No such clause turned up in TREC's own form list, in CE/study-guide material, or in any attorney/title-company commentary reviewed. This is consistent with the well-established, widely-taught fact among Texas practitioners that the standard TREC resale contract has no military-service, PCS, or deployment-specific termination provision — the contract's only no-cause exit is the negotiated option period (¶5), and its only for-cause exits are financing (Third Party Financing Addendum), title objections (¶6.D), casualty loss (¶4), appraisal (TREC 49-1 addendum), and the other named grounds enumerated on TREC Form 38-8 (Notice of Buyer's Termination of Contract).

**Finding confirmed: TREC 20-19 contains no military-specific termination language.** A buyer's available exits after going under contract are the same regardless of whether the change in circumstance is PCS orders, a job loss, or cold feet — the option period (if still open), a legitimate financing-contingency failure, an unresolved title objection, casualty loss, appraisal shortfall (if TREC 49-1 attached), or a separately negotiated addendum/amendment. Military orders do not add a new box to check on TREC 38-8.

## Corroborating secondary sources (used only to cross-check the primary-source reading, not as the basis of the legal conclusion)

- Congress.gov CRS Report R45283, "The Servicemembers Civil Relief Act (SCRA): Section-by-Section Summary" — general secondary confirmation that §3952 is protective/defensive in nature (shield against creditor/counterparty action), consistent with the direct statutory reading above.
- General web search across real-estate-law and military-legal-assistance sources (NC State Bar SCRA guide, Military OneSource SCRA overview, HSH.com "How the SCRA Protects Servicemembers") turned up no source asserting SCRA grants a purchase-contract cancellation right. No source contradicts the finding above.

## What did NOT change from the prior research pass

Everything in the prior pass held up under direct statutory review: §3955 = lease termination right on 90+ day PCS/deployment orders (confirmed, purchase contracts excluded by the text itself); §3952 = protects buyer against seller-side rescission, not a buyer cancellation right (confirmed by direct subsection (a)(1) language); TREC 20-19 has no military provision (confirmed by lack of any such clause turning up anywhere, including TREC's own form and third-party commentary).
