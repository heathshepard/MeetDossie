# Batch 22 citation trail — FHA/Fannie Mae/Freddie Mac Buyer-Agent Commission Guide

Guide: `marketing/guides-data/fha-fannie-freddie-buyer-agent-commission-guide.json`
(slug: `fha-fannie-freddie-buyer-agent-commission-guide`)

Topic: How seller-paid buyer-agent commissions interact with FHA/Fannie Mae/Freddie Mac
financing-concession (interested-party-contribution) limits, post-2024 NAR settlement — and
the "customary" conditional all three sources hang the exclusion on.

**The real story here is the conditional, not the rule.** All three sources exclude seller-paid
buyer-agent commissions from their concession/IPC caps ONLY as long as that payment remains
"customary" (Freddie's word), paid "as a manner of state and local law or custom" (FHA's word),
or paid "in accordance with local common and customary practices" (Fannie's word). None of the
three defines "customary" numerically, geographically, or with any trigger for when it stops
applying. That's a live risk, not a settled rule, in a market where the entire industry has been
renegotiating who pays buyer-agent comp since the settlement.

## Source 1 — FHA INFO 2024-12 (verified: primary PDF, fetched directly from HUD.gov)

**Document type — CONFIRMED, not a Mortgagee Letter.** It is an **"FHA INFO"** bulletin — HUD's
Office of Single Family Housing's industry-news publication (masthead reads "FHA SINGLE FAMILY
HOUSING INDUSTRY NEWS / FHA INFO"), not a numbered Mortgagee Letter (ML). FHA INFO items carry
their own separate numbering series and don't amend the Single Family Housing Policy Handbook the
way a Mortgagee Letter does — they're guidance/clarification bulletins.

- **Exact identifier:** FHA INFO 2024-12
- **Date:** March 28, 2024
- **Title:** "Frequently Asked Question on Seller-Paid Commissions Related to the National
  Association of REALTORS® Settlement"
- **Source URL:** https://www.hud.gov/sites/dfiles/SFH/documents/SFH_FHA_INFO_2024-12.pdf
  (fetched and read directly, full text below — not relayed from a secondary source)

**Full operative text, quoted verbatim:**

> "Under existing FHA policy, if sellers continue to pay buyer-side real estate agent commissions
> and fees as a manner of state and local law or custom, and if the commissions and fees are
> reasonable in amount, existing policy would not treat those payments as interested party
> contributions provided all other requirements are met."

Preceding sentence for context: "Since its announcement, FHA has received questions from its
stakeholders regarding how the proposed settlement agreement will affect the treatment of
seller-paid buyer real estate broker fees in transactions using FHA-insured mortgage financing."

Closing sentence — this is FHA's own hedge, worth quoting directly since it's the clearest of the
three sources about the conditional being live and unresolved:

> "FHA will continue to monitor the real estate marketplace for changes resulting from the
> settlement for potential impacts to its policies and will address additional questions as they
> develop."

**Two conditions stacked, both undefined:** (1) payment "as a manner of state and local law or
custom" and (2) the commissions/fees being "reasonable in amount." FHA INFO 2024-12 does not cite
a specific FHA Handbook section number for the underlying IPC policy it's clarifying, nor does it
define "reasonable in amount" with a percentage or dollar test.

## Source 2 — Fannie Mae Selling Notice (verified: primary PDF, fetched directly from
singlefamily.fanniemae.com)

- **Document type:** Selling Notice (not a Selling Guide update — the notice says so explicitly:
  "While there are no immediate changes to our Selling Guide policies, we are clarifying the
  current treatment...")
- **Date:** April 15, 2024
- **Title:** "Real Estate Commissions and Interested Party Contributions"
- **Source URL:** https://singlefamily.fanniemae.com/media/38706/display (fetched and read
  directly; the news-events landing page at
  singlefamily.fanniemae.com/news-events/selling-notice-real-estate-commissions-and-interested-party-contributions
  403'd on fetch, but the underlying PDF is the primary document and was read in full)

**Full operative paragraph, quoted verbatim:**

> "Selling Guide B3-4.1-02, Interested Party Contributions (IPCs) permits interested parties
> (including property sellers) to make contributions to the borrower's closing costs subject to
> maximum limits ranging between 2% and 9% of the property value. Typical fees and/or closing
> costs paid by a seller in accordance with local custom, known as common and customary fees or
> costs, are not subject to the IPC limits described in Selling Guide B3-4.1-03, Types of
> Interested Party Contributions (IPCs). If a seller or seller's real estate agent continues to
> pay the buyer's real estate agent commission in accordance with local common and customary
> practices, these amounts are not required to be counted towards the IPC limits for the
> transaction."

**IPC limit table (from B3-4.1-02, confirmed live on the current Selling Guide site):**

| Occupancy | LTV/CLTV | Max IPC |
|---|---|---|
| Principal residence / second home | >90% | 3% |
| Principal residence / second home | 75.01%–90% | 6% |
| Principal residence / second home | ≤75% | 9% |
| Investment property | all ratios | 2% |

This confirms the "2% to 9%" range referenced in the prior research pass and in the notice
itself.

**Citation drift flagged — read before republishing this guide after a Selling Guide update:**
the April 2024 notice cites "B3-4.1-03, Types of Interested Party Contributions (IPCs)" as the
section carrying the common-and-customary exclusion. Checked live against the CURRENT Fannie Mae
Selling Guide (fetched 2026-08-09): **B3-4.1-03 is now titled "Lender Incentives"** — a different
topic entirely — and the "common and customary fees or costs" exclusion language now lives inside
**B3-4.1-02** itself, alongside the IPC limit table. The guide's internal section numbering has
been reorganized since April 2024; the underlying substance (the exclusion, the 2–9% caps) is
still confirmed live, but a reader who goes looking for "B3-4.1-03" today to find the customary-
fees carve-out won't find it there anymore. The guide text should cite B3-4.1-02 as the current
home of both the limits and the exclusion, and flag B3-4.1-03 only as the original notice's
citation, not the current one.

## Source 3 — Freddie Mac FAQ (verified: primary PDF, fetched directly from sf.freddiemac.com)

- **Document type:** FAQ bulletin (single Q&A)
- **Date:** June 5, 2024
- **Title:** "Buyer Agent Commissions Paid by Property Sellers or Their Agents"
- **Source URL:** https://sf.freddiemac.com/docs/pdf/buyer-agent-commissions-faq.pdf (fetched and
  read directly, full text below)
- **Guide section referenced:** Section 5501.5

**Full Q&A, quoted verbatim:**

> "Q1: If the property seller or the property seller's real estate agent pays a fee to the
> buyer's real estate agent (i.e., the buyer's agent commission), is the amount of the fee
> subject to the financing concession limits in Guide Section 5501.5?
>
> A: As noted in Guide Section 5501.5, borrower fees or costs that are customarily paid by the
> property seller according to local convention are not subject to maximum financing concession
> limits. Buyer agent fees have historically been fees customarily paid by the property seller or
> property seller's real estate agent. **As long as this practice remains customary, buyer agent
> fees paid by the property seller or property seller's agent will continue to be excluded from
> financing concession limits.**"

This is the single most explicit statement of the conditional across all three sources — Freddie
says outright, in its own words, that the exclusion holds only "as long as this practice remains
customary." That sentence is the anchor for this guide's whole framing.

Note: this guide's title says "Buyer Agent Commissions," matching how Freddie itself refers to
the fee — not "buyer-agent compensation" or "cooperative compensation." Use Freddie's own term
when directly quoting or citing this source.

## Source 4 — TREC 20-19 ¶12C (verified: primary PDF, current promulgated contract form,
cross-checked against the already-verified quote in `new-trec-guides-citations-batch[N]` behind
`texas-trec-brokerage-compensation-2026-guide.json`)

Confirmed directly against the current TREC 20-19 PDF (`pdftotext` extraction of the primary
contract form, not relayed):

> "C. EXPENSE LIMITATION: If a governmental loan program regulation prohibits Buyer from paying
> charges or fees, then the amount of any concession in 12A(1)(b) shall be first applied to pay
> such prohibited expenses and then to other Buyer's Expenses but not brokerage compensation or
> contribution."

This is ¶12C exactly as the prior research pass described it, verbatim, and matches the quote
already published in `texas-trec-brokerage-compensation-2026-guide.json`. Confirms: TREC's
current form explicitly walls brokerage compensation and contributions out of the expense-
limitation reallocation — a seller's ¶12A(1)(b) concession, when a government loan program
(FHA/VA/USDA-type restriction) blocks the buyer from paying certain charges, gets redirected to
other Buyer's Expenses but never to brokerage compensation.

**How this connects to the FHA/Fannie/Freddie material:** ¶12C is a TREC contract mechanic, not a
loan-investor concession rule — it governs how a *seller's* TREC-contract concession gets
reallocated on the Texas contract itself when a government loan program won't let the buyer pay
certain fees. It does not independently create or remove FHA/Fannie/Freddie IPC treatment for
buyer-agent commissions. The two systems (TREC contract mechanics vs. loan-investor/insurer
concession-limit policy) are separate and this guide should not conflate them — ¶12C is included
because a reader drafting a TREC 20-19 contract needs to know brokerage compensation is walled off
from the ¶12A(1)(b) concession-reallocation mechanism regardless of what FHA/Fannie/Freddie do
with the commission on the financing side.

## What's NOT verified / explicitly flagged as ambiguous

1. **No numeric or geographic definition of "customary" exists in any of the three sources.**
   FHA says "state and local law or custom"; Fannie says "local custom" / "local common and
   customary practices"; Freddie says "customarily paid... according to local convention" and
   "as long as this practice remains customary." None ties this to a specific percentage of
   transactions, a specific MLS rule, or a sunset date. This guide must not imply a bright-line
   test exists — it doesn't.
2. **No Texas-specific customary-practice data was located or claimed.** This guide does not
   assert what percentage of Texas transactions currently have sellers paying buyer-agent
   commission, nor any TREC/Texas REALTORS® statement on whether the practice remains
   "customary" post-settlement. That would need a separate, dated source (e.g., a Texas
   REALTORS® market-practice survey) and none was located in this pass. Flagged as a gap — the
   guide frames this as an open risk, not with fabricated Texas-specific stats.
3. **FHA INFO 2024-12 does not cite a specific FHA Handbook (4000.1) section number** for the
   underlying IPC policy it clarifies — it references "existing FHA policy" generally. If a future
   pass wants to cite the specific Handbook provision, that needs separate verification against
   the current 4000.1 text; not attempted here.
4. **The Fannie Mae B3-4.1-03 section-number drift (see Source 2 above)** — confirmed as of
   2026-08-09 that B3-4.1-03 is now "Lender Incentives," not "Types of Interested Party
   Contributions" as the April 2024 notice cited. This guide cites B3-4.1-02 as the current home
   of the substance and notes the historical citation separately; it does not claim B3-4.1-03
   still covers this topic today.
5. **None of the three sources has been reissued, updated, or superseded as of this verification
   pass (2026-08-09)** — all three remain the most recent primary statements found on each
   respective agency's own site. No newer FHA INFO, Fannie Selling Notice, or Freddie FAQ on this
   specific topic was found. This should be re-checked before any future republish of this guide,
   given how directly the underlying "customary" premise depends on market conditions that have
   kept shifting since the settlement.
