The TREC Deadline Checklist

Every date-driven obligation in the One to Four Family Residential Contract (20-19), in order — what's printed into the form, what's a blank you filled in, and what it costs when you miss it.

By Heath Shepard, Texas REALTOR® · Updated 2026-09-30 · Source: TREC Form 20-19 (05-04-2026), the Third Party Financing Addendum, and the Lead-Based Paint Addendum
Scope: a standard residential resale under TREC 20-19 with a Third Party Financing Addendum attached. This is not legal advice and it does not replace reading your own executed contract — paragraph numbers and printed language can change when TREC revises a form. Always confirm against the contract you actually signed.

1. The First 3 Days
Clock starts the moment the contract is fully executed
Deliver earnest money & option fee
¶5.A
Buyer owes it
Within 3 days after the Effective Date, Buyer must deliver the earnest money and the option fee to the Escrow Agent. "3 days" is printed into the form — it is not a blank.
Miss it: under ¶5.C, Seller may terminate the contract (or pursue ¶15 default remedies) by giving Buyer notice before Buyer delivers the earnest money. Under ¶5.D, if the option fee isn't delivered on time — or no dollar amount was ever stated — Buyer never had the unrestricted right to terminate under ¶5B at all.
Additional earnest money (if negotiated)
¶5.A(1)
Buyer owes it — only if a second earnest money deposit was written into the contract
Buyer delivers the stated additional earnest money to Escrow Agent within ___ days after the Effective Date — a fillable blank, present only when the parties negotiated a second deposit.
Same consequence as the initial earnest money above — Seller's ¶5.C remedies apply.
⚠ Scope trap
Weekend / Legal Holiday rollover — ¶5.A deliveries ONLY
¶5.A(2)
Applies to: earnest money, option fee, additional earnest money
If the last day to deliver the earnest money, option fee, or additional earnest money falls on a Saturday, Sunday, or Legal Holiday (Tex. Gov't Code §662.003(a), (b)(4), (b)(6)), the deadline rolls to the next day that isn't one of those.
This rollover applies only to the three ¶5.A deliveries. It does not extend the ¶5.B option-period notice deadline, and it does not extend title, survey, financing, or closing deadlines — see the trap below.
2. The Option Period
The buyer's unrestricted, no-reason-needed right to walk
⚠ The most expensive trap in the form
Termination Option deadline
¶5.B
Buyer's right, Seller's exposure
Seller grants Buyer the unrestricted right to terminate by giving notice within ___ days after the Effective Date (Option Period) — a fillable blank. But: "Notices under this paragraph must be given by 5:00 p.m. (local time where the Property is located) by the date specified." The 5:00 p.m. cutoff is printed into the form. Only the number of days is a blank — the time of day is never negotiable text.
Miss 5:00 p.m. by even a minute and the unrestricted right to terminate is gone. The buyer is left with only cause-based exits (financing failure, uncured title objection, etc.) for the rest of the contract. The ___ days blank does not roll for weekends or holidays — that rollover (¶5.A(2)) applies only to the deliveries in ¶5.A, never to the ¶5.B notice deadline.
Failure to timely deliver the option fee
¶5.D
Buyer
"If no dollar amount is stated as the option fee or if Buyer fails to deliver the option fee within the time required, Buyer shall not have the unrestricted right to terminate this contract under this paragraph 5."
This is the gate in front of ¶5.B — the option right never vests if the fee itself was late or unstated.
⚠ No grace period
Time is of the essence
¶5.E
Both parties
"Time is of the essence for this paragraph and strict compliance with the time for performance is required." This sentence governs all of Paragraph 5 — earnest money, option fee, and the option period notice alike.
Courts read "time is of the essence" language literally. There is no implied reasonableness buffer anywhere in ¶5.
3. Disclosures
Two conditional windows, plus a federal requirement for older homes
Seller's Disclosure Notice (if not yet received)
¶7.B(2)
Seller delivers; Buyer's termination right follows
If Buyer checked "has not received," Seller must deliver within ___ days after the Effective Date (blank). If Buyer never receives it, Buyer may terminate at any time prior to closing. If Seller does deliver it, Buyer may terminate for any reason within 7 days after receipt or prior to closing, whichever occurs first — both "7 days" and "at any time prior to closing" are printed.
Miss the 7-day window after actual receipt (when Seller did deliver) and the for-any-reason right lapses — Buyer is back to ¶7A inspection-driven and option-period rights only.
Seller's Water Disclosure (if not yet received)
¶7.I(2)
Seller delivers; Buyer's termination right follows
Same structure as the Seller's Disclosure Notice above: Seller delivers within ___ days (blank) after Effective Date; if not received, Buyer may terminate any time prior to closing; if delivered, Buyer's for-any-reason window is 7 days after receipt or prior to closing, whichever first occurs.
Same consequence — the for-any-reason right expires 7 days after actual delivery.
Pre-1978 properties only
Lead-Based Paint disclosure & inspection window
Addendum 56-0, ¶C.2
Buyer's right; federal requirement (42 U.S.C. §4852d)
Unless Buyer waives this right entirely (¶C.1), Buyer has ten days after the effective date to have the Property inspected for lead-based paint. If paint or hazards are found, Buyer must give written notice within 14 days after the effective date to terminate. Both day counts are printed text on the addendum, not fillable blanks.
Two separate clocks, both running from the same effective date, both fixed. Missing the 14-day termination notice — even if the 10-day inspection happened on day 9 — forfeits the right to walk over what the inspection found.
4. Title & Survey
Usually the longest-running clock in the file
Title Commitment delivery
¶6.B
Seller furnishes (via Title Company)
Within 20 days after the Title Company receives a copy of the contract, Seller shall furnish the Commitment; Buyer pays for the Exception Documents. If not delivered in time, the deadline auto-extends "up to 15 days or 3 days before the Closing Date, whichever is earlier."
If the Commitment still isn't delivered after the extension, Buyer may terminate and the earnest money is refunded.
Survey
¶6.C
Depends which of 3 boxes is checked
Three mutually exclusive options, each on its own ___ days blank after the Effective Date: (1) Seller furnishes an existing survey + T-47 Affidavit; (2) Buyer obtains a new survey at Buyer's expense; (3) Seller furnishes a new survey at Seller's expense. Under option (1), if Seller fails to furnish both the existing survey and the affidavit in time, Buyer must obtain a new survey at Seller's expense no later than "3 days prior to Closing Date."
Confirm which box is actually checked on your executed contract before relying on this — the three options shift both the deadline and who pays.
Title objection deadline, cure period, and final window
¶6.D
Buyer objects; Seller cures; Buyer decides
Buyer must object in writing by the earlier of (i) the Closing Date, or (ii) ___ days (blank) after Buyer receives the Commitment, Exception Documents, and the survey — all three, not just one. Seller then has 15 days after receiving the objections to cure (Cure Period), and the Closing Date extends as necessary. If objections aren't cured, Buyer must deliver notice to terminate or waive within 5 days after the Cure Period ends.
Silence at the end of either window is a loss for Buyer: failing to object in time waives the objection right (except Schedule C items); failing to act within the 5-day post-cure window is a deemed waiver of the objection.
Flagged for Hadley — not independently verified HOA / POA Resale Certificate (Subdivision Information). If the Property is subject to mandatory POA membership, ¶6.E(2) of the 20-19 (verified against the specimen PDF) points the parties to Tex. Prop. Code §207.003 and to "the TREC promulgated Addendum for Property Subject to Mandatory Membership in a Property Owners Association" (TREC 36-11) for the actual delivery deadline and Buyer's termination window. I do not have a blank specimen of TREC 36-11 in scripts/trec-forms/ to verify its paragraph lettering directly — two internal reference docs in this repo (data/hadley-knowledge/trec-36-11.md and docs/hadley-pass-report-trec-36-11-2026-07-01-updated.md) disagree with each other on which letter (¶A vs ¶B, ¶C vs ¶D) carries the delivery-days blank and the termination-days blank, which is exactly why I'm not printing specific paragraph citations or day counts here. Do not publish specific 36-11 paragraph letters until someone checks them against the actual form. What's safe to say: both deadlines are fillable blanks set when the addendum is drafted, not fixed printed text.
5. Financing
Third Party Financing Addendum (TREC 40-9) — only if attached
Buyer Approval notice deadline
TPFA ¶2.A
Buyer
If the box "subject to Buyer obtaining Buyer Approval" is checked, Buyer must give written notice to Seller within ___ days (blank) after the effective date if Buyer cannot obtain Buyer Approval, or the contract stops being contingent on it. "Time is of the essence for this paragraph and strict compliance with the time for performance is required" — printed, governing all of ¶2.
Miss the notice window and the financing contingency itself lapses — Buyer is treated as having obtained approval whether or not that's true.
Property Approval termination deadline
TPFA ¶2.B
Buyer
If the lender determines the Property doesn't satisfy underwriting requirements, Buyer may terminate "not later than 3 days before the Closing Date," by giving Seller notice of termination plus a copy of the lender's written statement of reasons. "3 days" is printed, fixed to the Closing Date — not a blank, not a fixed calendar date either (it moves if the Closing Date moves).
If Buyer doesn't terminate in time, Property Approval is deemed obtained — the contingency is gone regardless of the lender's actual position.
6. Closing Runway
The last obligations before the Closing Date itself
Completion of agreed repairs & treatments
¶7.F
Seller
Unless otherwise agreed in writing, Seller completes all agreed repairs/treatments prior to the Closing Date. If Seller fails, Buyer may exercise ¶15 default remedies or "extend the Closing Date up to 5 days if necessary" for Seller to finish.
The 5-day extension is Buyer's option, not automatic — it has to be invoked, not assumed.
Closing Date
¶9.A
Both parties
Closing occurs on or before the date stated in the contract, or "within 7 days after objections made under Paragraph 6D have been cured or waived, whichever date is later." The 7-day extension is printed, automatic when title objections push past the stated date.
Failing to close by the operative Closing Date puts the non-defaulting party into ¶15 default remedies — specific performance or termination with liquidated damages.

One amendment moves the whole chain

The current Amendment to Contract (TREC 39-11) lets the parties change the Closing Date directly — ¶(3) — extend the option period with an additional fee and a new 5:00 p.m. date — ¶(7) — or move the Buyer Approval notice deadline under the Financing Addendum — ¶(9).

Several deadlines above are written relative to the Closing Date, not a fixed calendar date: the ¶6.C(1) survey fallback ("3 days prior to Closing Date"), the Financing Addendum's ¶2.B Property Approval window ("3 days before the Closing Date"), and ¶6.D's "earlier of the Closing Date" title-objection backstop all move automatically the moment ¶9.A's date changes. A single signed 39-11 changing the Closing Date silently rebuilds three other deadlines that nobody re-typed.
